4.2 Results and discussion
4.2.1 Computational details

Unlike the other chapters in this thesis, the research presented here is not related
to methodology development. We have not proposed any method or implemented
any code; rather, we have performed a large amount of quantum chemicals calcu-

lations, and carried out a large scale analysis of the results.

We have performed electronic structure calculations on ~ 200 molecules, both
organic and inorganic, covering the majority of most typical functional groups in or-
ganic chemistry. These comprise saturated and unsaturated (including several aro-
matic) hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, acids (and deriva-
tives), amines, sulphides, several heterocyclic derivatives (of pyrrole and thiophene,
as well as the nitrogenated bases), cyanides and isocyanides, ethers, diazocom-
pounds and nitro-derivatives, and additionally the most common amino acids. This
set also contains 28 simple inorganic molecules, setting what is, to our knowledge,
the most complete delocalization index study to date. A comprehensive list of com-

pounds is provided in Appendix A.2.

The calculations were carried out with GAUSSIAN 09 [235], using the Pople
6-311++G(2d,2p) basis set, and either the Hartree-Fock or CCSD (Coupled Clus-
ter theory with Single and Double excitations) levels of theory. The majority of
reported data corresponds to Hartree-Fock theory, which will be assumed unless
otherwise stated. The calculated wavefunctions were then employed to calculate
QTAIM descriptors, including the delocalization index between each pair of quan-
tum atoms, employing AIMAII [236].

bitals, the eigenvectors of the 1-RDM [161]; no information about the 2-RDM is
outputted. For this reason, and for the computational expense and complexity of

the calculations. We provide a benchmark below, that employs the pySCF suite
