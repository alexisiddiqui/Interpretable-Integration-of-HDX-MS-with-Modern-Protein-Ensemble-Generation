There are several other alternatives that rely on similar principles, such as the
CHELP method, employing a Lagrange multiplier approach [70] and the CHELPG
method which combines the other two [71]. Especially important is the RESP (Re-
strained ElectroStatic Potential) method of Bayly et al [72], which generalises the
method of Cox and Williams to include several regularisation constraints that pre-
vent nonphysically large charges and reduce the conformational dependence of the
charges. This method has been employed in the widely extended AMBER force
field, and can be considered one of the most popular [63].

2.2 Intermolecular forces

2.2.1 Introduction and historical context

In order to understand the weaknesses of force fields regarding non-bonded inter-
actions, this section will be concerned with the theory of intermolecular interac-
tions. Ordinarily, general chemistry textbooks provide a very simplified picture of
these forces, that is limited to classify molecules as polar or non-polar and induce
what kind of interactions should the condensed phase experience. However, inter-

molecular forces are a rich and insightful area of study.
