Here, ip; are the occupied orbitals (CPMD is conceived for one-electron theories,
such as DFT or Hartree-Fock), and the constraints include orbital orthonormality
or, if necessary, thermostats, barostats, etc. The electronic degrees of freedom are
propagated under appropriate conditions to ensure that the system remains in the

ground state.

A major role is played by the parameter y, representing a fictitious mass for
electronic degrees of freedom. On one hand, this parameter controls the adiabatic
separation of electronic and nuclear subsystems, preventing a transference of en-
ergy between them that might take the system out of the ground state. On the
other, 1 is directly related to the maximum allowed timestep [33]. For this reason, it
is necessary to find an optimal value for each simulation, and a significant amount

of literature has been devoted to study this problem [51, 52, 53, 54].

Applying the Euler-Lagrange approach to obtain the equations of motion, it can
be seen that these are very similar to the BOMD; in fact, they become the latter as
pz — 0.
