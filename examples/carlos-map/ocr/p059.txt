2.4.1 The Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules

The Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules is a paradigm in molecular quantum
chemistry proposed by Richard F. W. Bader in the seventies and eighties [140]. The
central point to this theory is that it is possible to define a partition of real space
IR° by means of a set of interatomic surfaces (IAS), that are defined by the zero flux

condition:

Vp-n=0 (2.91)

The regions {O,,} defined by this partition are termed atomic basins. Their con-
nection to the intuitive concept of atom is made evident by the theory of dynamical
systems: the electron density approaches a maximum close to the nuclear positions
(mathematically there is a singularity, although in practice there is a real value).
Therefore, the nuclei act as attractors of the electron density, and the IAS are simply

the regions that separate the territory of two attractors.

p(r), the electron density. A given critical point can be classified by means of the
eigenvalues of its Hessian, in terms of its rank w (number of non-zero eigenvalues)
and signature o (the sum of the signs of the eigenvalues). Since only the points with
