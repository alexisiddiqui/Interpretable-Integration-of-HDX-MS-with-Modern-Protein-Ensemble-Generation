A theory that allows to recover the division of a molecule in atoms, maintaining
the classical chemical vision while ensuring that all molecular and atomic proper-
ties remain as Dirac observables was proposed by Richard F. W. Bader in the sev-
enties, under the name of Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM). This
theory is based on the division of real space IR° in several atoms, which constitute

open quantum systems.

While not enjoying the same quantum mechanical rigour, other theories propos-
ing a partition of real space have been proposed, including the Electron Localiza-
tion Function (ELF) [135] and Hirshfeld partitioning [136], to name a few. These
theories have been widely considered to form part of a family of techniques called
Quantum Chemical Topology (QCT), a term proposed by Prof. Popelier in 2003, in a
quite extended footnote [137]. However, while the term was coined by Popelier, the
conception of these theories as topological analysis was well-extended in the commu-
nity before; proof of this is the presence of titles as “Classification of chemical bonds
based on topological analysis of electron localization functions”, the Nature paper
by Silvi and Savin in 1994 [138].

The name topology is a consequence of the atomic partitioning inducing a topol-

ogy in real space, in the usual mathematical sense [139]:

Definition 1 (Topology) A topology on a set X is a collection (i. e. a set whose elements
are sets) T of subsets of X having the following properties:

1. © (the empty set) and X are in T,
2. The union of the elements of any subcollection of T is in T,

3. The intersection of the elements of any finite subcollection of T is in T.
