At first sight, the strategy we have proposed might seem similar to the naive ap-
proach, described in the introduction, of increasing the number of conformational
seeds. Our approach is however quite different. Since we are projecting the training
set using the representation matrices, the new points do not need to be calculated,
simplifying the training. More importantly, the symmetry breakings due to integra-

tion errors are avoided.

It is key to realise that symmetry-adapted kriging is not equivalent to ordinary
kriging with projected points. In our formulation, the weights for all the symmetry-

equivalent are identical, a condition that does not hold for ordinary kriging, where:

The major drawback of symmetry-adapted kriging is that it does not actually re-
duce the number of evaluations. Since the symmetry-adapted kernel ¢(r) requires
N evaluations, then the computational scaling of the method scales as O(NM)
where M is the number of training points. The asymptotic complexity is linear,
but it is paramount to reduce the factor M as much as possible. Fortunately, it turns
out that it is possible to reduce the number of evaluations by a factor close to N by

employing clever heuristics.
