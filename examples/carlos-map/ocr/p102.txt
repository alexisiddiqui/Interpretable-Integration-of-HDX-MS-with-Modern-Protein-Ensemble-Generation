3.4.1 The challenge of polarisation

We will first assess the lack of polarisation in FFLUX, which is a problem in its
physical description of intermolecular interactions, rather than a technical prob-
lem related to the details of the parametrization. This arises from one of the major
assumptions of the force field: that the charge distribution depends only on the
molecular geometry, and that charge transfer effects are governed only by confor-

mational changes.

Turning to the electrostatic interaction described in equation 3.2, one might eas-
ily observe that the charges (and higher-order multipoles) on every atomic site are
obtained from the kriging models, which depend on the molecular geometry alone.
There is, in principle, no direct influence of the environment: two molecules in com-
pletely different environments will show the same charges, as long as both share
the same geometry. In other words, the environment affects the molecular charge

distribution only by distorting the geometry.

This assumption means that, although FFLUX has been presented as a polaris-
able force field, in rigour it can only be categorised as a multipolar force field, since
the electrostatics do not respond directly to the changes in the environment. In
other words, the polarisation is intramolecular rather than intermolecular, in contrast
to the polarisable force fields presented in Section 2.3, where both forms are present.
For example, in fluctuating charge force fields, the intermolecular response is repre-
sented by a charge redistribution until the electronegativity constraints are fulfilled.
Similarly, in inducible dipole force fields, this is accounted explicitly employing
Applequist model. In both cases, two molecules with the same geometry will have
different charge distributions if their environments differ.
