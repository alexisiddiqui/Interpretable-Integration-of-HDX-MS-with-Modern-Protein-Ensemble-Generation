In Figures 5.9 and 5.10 we show a representative example of the large body of
methanol simulations, respectively for the baseline and symmetry-adapted case. It

is possible to find examples of all the behaviours that are described above.

The experiment in acetaldehyde is shown in Figure 5.11 for runs without (top)
and with symmetry-adapted kriging (bottom). As in the previous case, we find that
the baseline runs converge to several distinct wrong structures, while symmetry-

adapted runs converge readily to the expected minimum.

First, we note that the RMSD in this case are magnified with respect to the previ-
ous example. In methanol, most of the RMSD is accounted by the hydroxyl group;
however, in acetaldehyde the hydroxyl group is substituted by the O=C-H moiety.
When two structures are superimposed, there will now be two atoms that do not
coincide significantly with the reference, and thus the RMSD will be significantly
higher. This also justifies the clustering of runs in the rightmost plot’, since the
small differences in atomic positions after permutating the coordinates are magni-
fied.

Regardless of the last effect, it seems that the conclusions for acetaldehyde are
quite similar: all the symmetry-adapted runs converge to the expected minimum,
while the baseline show several wrong structures. Particularly, the proper config-
urations BCA and CAB also pass close to the minimum before drifting away. It is

also seen that the improper rotations converge generally to senseless structures.
