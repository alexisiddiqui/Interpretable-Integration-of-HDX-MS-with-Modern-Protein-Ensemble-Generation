Chapter 2

Literature review

OMPUTATIONAL chemistry is a multidisciplinary field that involves and
brings together chemistry, physics, mathematics and computer science,
among others. Any attempt to completely map such extensive territory is

deemed to be futile, and even ambitious projects, such as the celebrated Encyclopedia
of Computational Chemistry [21], are doomed to become somewhat obsolete shortly

after their publication.

In all awareness of this difficulty, we have attempted to reflect the state-of-the-
art of computational chemistry in the most sensible way possible. This review fo-
cuses on the branches of computational chemistry that are directly related to the
research described in further chapters, and try to balance the basic theory to the

latest advances. The structure of the text reflects these concerns.

In this way, section 2.1 attempts to connect the basic concepts of quantum chem-
istry with the simulation in terms of molecular mechanics potentials. This is done
by sequentially introducing approximations to Schrédinger equations until clas-
sical force fields are derived. Then, the inadequacy of the typical intermolecular
terms leads to a discussion of the theory of intermolecular forces in section 2.2, and
later a review of a particularly successful improvement on the classical formulation,

polarisable force fields, in section 2.3.
