In Figure 5.8 we show the evolution of the minimisation along 1500 timesteps,
for runs without (top) and with symmetry-adapted kriging (bottom). At first sight,
it is clear that the baseline runs converge to several distinct wrong structures, while
symmetry-adapted runs converge readily to the expected minimum. The mean
final RMSD to the B3LYP/aug-cc-pVTZ optimum is 0.002+0.004 A.

There are some interesting features in the baseline curves. First, all the ABC runs
converge to the right minimum, as it would be expected: they are the runs in the
well-modelled symmetry region Or. It is also noteworthy that a large amount of
the proper rotations BCA and CAB get initially very close to the expected minimum
and then drift away. In these runs, the hydroxyl group rotates until reaching the
expected minimum and then, since the potential does not recognise a minimum,
continues rotating, ignoring the rotation barrier, until reaching the ABC minimum,

which is the only one where the potential acknowledges a minimum.

The improper rotations do not experience this behaviour, and generally the
methyl group twists while the hydrogen atoms try to reassemble to invert the hand-
edness in a completely unphysical evolution. In most of the cases, these runs get

stuck in a meaningless structure due to the extrapolation collapse.

Finally, we note that there is an ACB (pink) simulation that experiences an er-
ratic behaviour with a kink around 200 fs. We have analysed this trajectory care-
fully, and concluded that it is a boundary effect. Since kriging is a non-linear inter-
polation method, the potential in the borders of the training set is significantly less
accurate that in regions are surrounded by training points. Therefore, if the simula-
tion begins close to this boundary, as is the case for this particular trajectory, the ini-
tial forces can be completely wrong. In this case, the simulation begins quite close to
the boundary, and receives a significant force that pushes it through the boundary
between two symmetry-equivalent regions Ox and Oy. As a consequence, there is
a small period of readjustment that reminds of a damped oscillation, until finally

the structure is within the training set and converges to a minimum.
