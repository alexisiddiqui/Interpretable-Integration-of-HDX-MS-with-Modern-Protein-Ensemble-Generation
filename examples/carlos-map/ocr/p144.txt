4.4 Conclusions

In this chapter we have presented the delocalization index, a magnitude that
measures the amount of delocalized electron pairs between two atoms, and that
is a first-order approximation (equivalent to a charge-charge interaction) of the
exchange-correlation energy. This project started as an study of a cheap exchange
model for the peptide bond in realistic simulations of FFLUX, but the delocalization

index soon proved to be a very good indicator, changing the objective of the project.

We have analysed a very large list of compounds, comprising almost 200 molecules,
that is to our knowledge the last ever surveyed in these terms. After performing a
reduced benchmark to demonstrate the qualitative effectiveness (about a 5% rela-
tive error for directly bonded atoms) of Miiller approximation to the CCSD second-
order reduced density matrix, we have analysed a number of chemical effects in the

dataset.

One of the most important results of our study is that the delocalization index
can offer a natural clustering of values that is reminiscent of the well-known single,
double and triple bonds, connecting the quantitative power of quantum chemistry
with the simple concepts found in textbook Lewis’ diagrams. The bond orders sug-
gested by Hartree-Fock delocalization indices are the closest to the classical, formal
bond orders. The inclusion of Coulomb correlation alters the values, which are now
not trivially close to the expected values; however, we have provided significant ar-
gumentation to show that this is a consequence of a better understanding of the

bonding, rather than a failure of the theory.
