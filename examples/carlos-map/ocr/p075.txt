We will now work on each of the terms in this equation until we obtain a simple
expression that allows to perform training and prediction using only methods from
linear algebra that can be easily implemented in a computer. However, it is worth
to stop briefly on equation 2.143, and appreciate why Gaussian processes are so

important.

Although in the general case an infinite-dimensional Gaussian process can rep-
resent any function, there is no reason to assume it is the best model. In fact, they
are inherently bad to model certain processes, with financial data, plagued by sharp

discontinuities, being a good example [190].

However, Gaussian process have a significant advantage that makes them widely
used even when they are not the most appropriate model: they are analytically
tractable. For most of the probability distributions, evaluating integrals such as
equation 2.143 is extremely involved and requires expensive techniques such as
Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), or severe approximations such as variational
inference [191]. Hereafter, we will assume that a Gaussian model is adequate, as is

probably true for the majority of problems related to the objective of this thesis.
