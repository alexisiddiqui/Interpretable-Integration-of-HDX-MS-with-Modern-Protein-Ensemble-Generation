3.4 Accritical appraisal

Every model of physical reality is subject to errors, and understanding when and
why these errors are important is perhaps as crucial as employing them correctly.
This might be clarified with a simple example. Hartree-Fock theory is very success-
ful at predicting geometries and thermochemical properties of organic compounds;
however, employing this same method to metallic complexes will lead to disas-
trous conclusions. This does not imply that the theory itself is wrong: it means that
metallic complexes are beyond its range of applicability. Similarly, understanding
the reasons behind the failure, in this case the existence of static correlation, allows

to design more powerful methods that can cope with new problems.

Here, after thoroughly presenting the formulation of FFLUX, we will try to dis-
cuss some of its weaknesses. In this context, these shall not be understood as a
criticism to the force field, but rather an understanding of its limitations that allows
to consider its development and applications carefully. As an example, the curse of
dimensionality presented in Section 3.4.3 implies that handling proteins will only
be possible after introducing severe approximations in the models. However, this
same difficulty does not apply to water molecules, and perhaps FFLUX should be
focused in producing a cheap and accurate model of solvation, a development long

awaited by the quantum chemical community [238].

The difficulties presented here will be based in physical and mathematical rea-
sonings, and will also be exemplified with some simple numerical verifications. It is
not the main objective of this thesis, or of this chapter, to treat these problems in de-
tail, and so the discussion will be kept as concise and simple as possible. Similarly,
the examples are brief and self-contained, although it will become evident that they

are representative of the respective phenomena, rather than anecdotal findings.
