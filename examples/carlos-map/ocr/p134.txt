Regarding carbon-nitrogen bonds, displayed in lower left plot of Figure 4.1, we
encounter the plateau of the single C-N bond in differently substituted amines, and
also several intermediate points, such as the C-N in amides and resonant heterocy-
cles, and less common double and triple bonds. Finally, the carbon-hydrogen plot
shows a unique plateau, around 0.8-1.0, as expected from the rather dull carbon-
hydrogen bonds. The only outlier, at 0.5, corresponds to a particularly strong through-
space interaction between two atoms in the aminoacid lysine, that are not directly
bonded according to classical Lewis theory, but show a bond critical point connect-

ing them at Hartree-Fock level.

Note that polar bonds with multiple bonding (as do ionic compounds, under-
represented in this study) show a particularly significant difference between their
delocalization indices and the bond orders expected from Lewis picture; for ex-
ample, a carbon-carbon double bound has an average delocalization index of 1.8,
while its carbon-oxygen analogous is about 1.2-1.4. This phenomenon, already
commented on by Fradera et al. [254] almost two decades ago, is a result of un-
equal sharing of electrons due to different electronegativities (note that the delo-
calization index measures equally shared electron pairs). Although Fradera et al.
suggested to augment the delocalization analysis by means of orbital arguments,
the results shown thus far confirm that the former can provide model-independent

information and go beyond naive Lewis bond orders.

These results lead to the conclusion that there are several very stable bonding
patterns, which correlate with the classical vision of single, double and triple bonds
as represented by Lewis structures, as well as aromatic intermediates. However,
considering many cases reveals that bond order is actually a continuous distribu-

tion, confirming and then extending Lewis’ picture
