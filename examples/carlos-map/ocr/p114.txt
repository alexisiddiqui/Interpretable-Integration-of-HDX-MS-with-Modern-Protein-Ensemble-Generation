Additionally, the sampling concerns only one of the many possible conformers
of glycine. It is well-known that the amount of possible conformers grows expo-
nentially with the number of rotatable bonds [247]. Considering that the current
model requires 0.5 GB of examples to predict the energetics of one conformer only,
the size of the model required to explore the full conformational space will be very
large. This is not only important in terms of the tens of thousands of exponentials
that will be evaluated for every predicted magnitude in every atom — it will also

imply a significant consumption of the available memory.

The curse of dimensionality is also the reason why the optimisation of larger
examples, such as capped alanine and capped serine, which were recently tested
thoroughly in our group, suffer spontaneous dissociation due to the extrapolation
collapse, as in a high-dimensional space it is very easy to leave the training set. Fu-
ture work should be devoted to retest the validity of kriging models against test
sets that are representative of the actual geometries found during a simulation.
Moreover, methodologies should be implemented to reduce the number of training
points as much as possible, in order to avoid a catastrophic exponential increase of

the computational resources during a simulation.

ing non-parametric regression models for the valence potential, as well as high-
order multipolar electrostatics with flexible multipoles. We have also reported the
derivation of the atomic forces in some detail, and described the parametrization
pipeline.
