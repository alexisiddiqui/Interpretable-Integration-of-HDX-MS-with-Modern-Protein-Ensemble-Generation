Of greater importance are the bond critical points. As their name indicates, these

The following step in our presentation of QTAIM is to define the concept of
atomic observable; to do this, however, we need to ensure that the systems defined
by the zero flux condition described above are quantum-mechanically consistent.
Following a recent preprint on the open problems of QTAIM [152], there are three
main ways of deriving this fact (and the zero flux condition): through the unique-
ness of the kinetic energy (however, it has been pointed out that not all families
of kinetic energy operators will yield the same expectation value if integrated over
a QTAIM basin [153]), through the atomic virial theorem and through the varia-
tional principles. All this methods are described to some extent in Bader’s original

monograph [140].

In this section, we have decided to employ the variational formulation for its
particular elegance. We note, however, that the use of Schwinger’s action principle
as done by Bader has been criticised by Cassam-Jenai and Jayatilaka due to math-
ematical problems [154, 155, 156]. Here, we follow the approach of Pendas et al.
[157], which is more mathematically elegant in our view, and does not suffer of this
problem, since it does not employ Schwinger’s action principle. For a mathemati-

cally rigorous derivation, we refer to the work of Shahbazian [158].
