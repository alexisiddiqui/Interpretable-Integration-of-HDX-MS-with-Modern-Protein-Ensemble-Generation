The objective of our initial experiment is as follows. We will stretch one of the
bonds of our molecule (for example, O1-H2 in water), and train a kriging model to
reproduce this dissociation curve. Then, we will try to obtain this same curve ina
different bond (in water, O1-H3), and observe the result. Since ordinary kriging is
not symmetry-invariant, we should obtain a rather senseless result, due to the ex-
trapolation collapse; however, symmetry-adapted kriging should return the same

curve.

In order to build the dissociation curve, the geometries of water and ammo-
nia were optimised at the MP2/cc-pVTZ level of theory employing GAUSSIAN 09
[235]. Then, the X;—Hz (where X=O, N) bonds were stretched in the range 0.7-1.2
A in H2O and 0.7-1.3 A in NH3. A hundred MP2/cc-pVTZ single point calculations
were performed for each molecule, creating a training set that was provided to the

MolecularSAGPR model, as well as the corresponding permutation matrices.

The results are displayed for water in Figure 5.5 and for ammonia in Figure 5.6.
