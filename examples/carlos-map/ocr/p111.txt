Building a kriging model is in a sense not very different from numerically evalu-
ating an integral: in both cases, we are covering a N-dimensional space with obser-
vations. The only difference is that, instead of employing the weighted sum, we are
interpolating the closest points to produce a prediction. In the same way that nu-
merically evaluating a 50D integral seems barely reasonable, there are also several

limitations to the kriging models that can be produced.

The curse of dimensionality and its consequences are well-known in statistics
and machine learning [244]. We will not provide a complete mathematical discus-
sion of its consequences and computational complexity, which are well beyond the
scope of this thesis. We will simply show how even state-of-the-art models of very
large size are unable to produce reasonable predictions. The experiment shown
here is particularly simple: we will employ the latest published model, capped
glycine [219], and show that it produces absolutely unreasonable responses in per-

fectly reasonable glycine conformations.
