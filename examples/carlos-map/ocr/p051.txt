Additive force fields are generally parametrized using a mean-field approxima-
tion: they respond in an average way to different environments. However, this
does not allow to describe a variety of systems, where a finer description of the
physical reality must be achieved to obtain the right answers for the right reasons.
The branch of polarisable force fields is based on the perhaps incremental approach
of allowing the electrostatics of a molecular system to vary as a response to the

environment.

In this section, we offer a review of the main literature in polarisable force fields
during the last two decades. We recognise the three major branches: fluctuating
charges, Drude oscillators, and inducible dipoles. Our review is far from compre-

hensive, and aims to contextualise rather than enumerate.

We will focus on the physical treatment of polarisation, and obviate other details
such as parametrization (which, in many cases can change significantly, even within
a branch) or applications. This information can be accessed in a number of reviews,
both of polarisable force fields in general [13, 103, 106, 107], and of the particular
classes [108, 109].

2.3.2 Fluctuating charges

The fluctuating charge model for polarisable force fields is perhaps one of the sim-
plest ideas: given the lack of transferability of fixed charges, these are allowed to
fluctuate on the basis of a physical mechanism. The latter is grounded on the idea
of electronegativity equalisation [110], that the charge in a molecule is redistributed to

equalise the electronegativity at each point.
