In order to model macromolecules, we need to incorporate a new term into FFLUX.

There are several possible approaches to achieve an appropriate model of the
peptide bond. First, one might employ an appropriately parameterised harmonic
potential between the aminoacids. This would in principle ensure the cohesion of
the peptide chain, but it is undesirable, both conceptually and technically, to spoil
the careful development of FFLUX by introducing the same coarse potentials that
it aimed to supersede. On the other hand, we might do just the opposite: introduce
a potential that explicitly includes the IQA exchange-correlation energy, which we

defined in Section 2.4.2 to be:

Vac(A,B) = I P1(11)P2(t2) — P2(t1, 12) dridry (4.1)

There are several problems in this formulation. Computing V;- requires a costly
six-dimensional integration in a complicated boundary; this also requires important
approximations, such as Miiller’s approximation described vide infra or the ones
discussed in Section 2.4.3. Moreover, since we are fitting a potential dependent on
the coordinates of two aminoacids, the model will require an enormous amount of
points due to the curse of dimensionality. Concerns of kriging performance aside,
the process of generating a training set with the full exchange-correlation energy

seems too costly to be reasonable.

A possible solution passes by approximating the exchange-correlation term by
another magnitude: the delocalization index. It was proved by Popelier for Hartree-
Fock theory that the delocalization index is a first order approximation to Vy¢ [248]
that requires only three-dimensional integrals; we will actually generalise this result
to arbitrary wavefunctions in Section 4.1.2. Moreover, this magnitude has been
applied in several theoretical studies, and is generally accepted as a bond order

indicator within the Quantum Chemical Topology community [249].
