A significant amount of work has been dedicated to produce novel force fields,
mostly through the perhaps incremental approach of including polarisation effects
[13, 14]. Moreover, in a very recent perspective by Teresa Head-Gordon [15], it was

claimed that:

Looking further ahead, force fields may undergo a more radical transfor-
mation, moving away from certain privileged functional forms selected
based on physical observations. Machine learning opens up the possi-
bility of simulating interaction energies and forces without any sort of
explicit functional form, or at least of automatically selecting functional

forms.

With the advent of deep learning [16], several very successful models have
arisen, for example for prediction of thermochemical quantities [17] or as full-fledged
force fields [18].

The Popelier group has been amongst the pioneers employing machine learn-
ing for molecular simulation, since more than a decade ago [19]. This approach, the
Quantum Chemical Topological Force Field (QCTFF, later on renamed as FFLUX)
employs a non-parametric potential based on knowledgeable quantum atoms [20],
that has a strong focus on employing multipolar electrostatics to provide an accu-

rate modelling of intermolecular interactions.
