4.3.2 A molecule with planar tetracoordinate carbon

If the previous molecule defied Lewis’ rules, the following system will challenge
another basic principle: the tetrahedral coordination of carbon, which is one of
the milestones of organic chemistry since the early work of van’t Hoff and Le Bel
[292, 293, 294]. This violation is far from new, and planar tetracoordinate carbon

compounds have been widely studied in the last fifty years [295, 296].

Here we address an example of planar tetracoordinate carbon: a cyclopropane
where both of the hydrogens attached to a carbon atom have been substituted by
lithium. This compound was discovered by Sorger and Schleyer in 1995 [297], and
we have selected it for its simplicity and small size. The optimised structures, to-

gether with the delocalization indices, are displayed in Figure 4.7.

The delocalization indices agree with the expected features of the molecule.
First, all carbon-carbon bonds are clearly single, their values ranging between 0.98
and 1.11. The C3-C1 and C3-C2 bonds show a significant enrichment in compari-
son with the C1-C2 bond. This is explained by the inductive effect of the C3, with a
large negative charge of -1.42 e that is in turn induced by the two atoms of lithium,
a highly electropositive element.
