One of the main advantages of the fluctuating charges method is that it intro-
duces no new potentials, and thus the implementation is easy and can take advan-
tage of previous software development. There are also efficient methods to propa-
gate the charges in an extended Lagrangian fashion [103, 108], although the smaller

timesteps required can burden computation nonetheless.

Nevertheless, the fluctuating charges method has some intrinsic problems. As a
start, the charges represent only the first moment in the multipole expansion, and
do not represent the electron distribution accurately in general [117]. In this way,
fluctuating charges force fields include polarisation corrections, but their treatment

of electrostatics is no better than in additive force fields.

There are also more technical problems. On one hand, it has been shown that
out-of-plane polarisation for planar or linear moieties is not reproduced, since the
charge transfer proceeds along the bonds [103]. Additionally, it was shown that
fluctuating charges cannot reproduce the energy of alanine tetrapeptide conform-
ers [115], which has prompted a migration of research to the induced dipoles frame-
work [103] described vide infra.

There are several current implementations of the fluctuating charge model. Some
of the most extended include the universal force field UFF [118], and a version of
CHARMM22 [119, 120], as well as the original TIP4P/FQ [111] model. There is also
a generalisation of the method to fluctuating multipoles [121].
