Regardless of their minimality, and the elegant quantum-mechanical formula-
tion of QTAIM multipoles, they are endowed of certain attractive properties that

will make them a key part of the research described in this thesis.

One of the features of topological multipoles is that their magnitude increases
rapidly with the rank, with high-rank moments being usually larger than the over-
all molecular moment [87]. This was initially considered a problem; however,
Kosov and Popelier showed that AIM multipoles were adequate to expand the elec-
trostatic potential [177], and that in fact the decay of the electron density favours a

fast convergence [178].

Further studies discovered that, while the convergence of AIM multipoles is in-
ferior to DMA multipoles for small systems, such as water, the former have superior

properties in larger systems [179] such as DNA [180] or crambin [181].

Of comparable importance is the fact that topological multipoles are virtually
basis set independent [182, 183], in contrast to DMA multipoles. This indepen-
dence arises from the real space integration, which is also the source of their main
disadvantages when compared with analytical multipoles: the significant time de-

manded by computation [87], and the existence of small integration errors.
