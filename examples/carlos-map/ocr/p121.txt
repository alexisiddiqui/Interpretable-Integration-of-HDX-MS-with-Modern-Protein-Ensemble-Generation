The terms A(A) and 6(A,B) are known respectively as the localization and de-
localization indices. From their definition, it is evident that the electron pairs of an
atom can thus be split into two subsets: those pairs that are unshared, localized in
the atom, and those that are shared or delocalized between that atom and the rest.
This allows to link the Lewis picture of electron sharing with electron population

analyses built on wavefunctions.

As mentioned in the introduction, delocalization indices are hardly new to the
QCT community; the concept was introduced by Bader and Stephens as early as
1975 [253], and its connection with the Lewis’ model has already been suggested
for covalent molecules [254], although with some problems’. DIs have been used in
the literature for some time [255, 256, 257, 258, 259], and there exist some general-
isations such as the n-order DIs [260, 261] and localization-delocalization matrices
[262, 263]; a particularly beautiful application is the monitoring of the delocaliza-
tion indices along a reaction path, that provides information about the nature of the
underlying chemical change [264, 265, 266, 267]. In fact, Fulton already proposed
in 1993 an expression for bond order that is equivalent to the delocalization index

computed within Miiller’s approximation described below [268].

We note that everything discussed to this point is experimentally observable in
principle. Since the exchange-correlation density is the expectation value of a Her-
mitian operator (in the theoretical jargon, it is a Dirac observable), then according
to the postulates of quantum mechanics it can be measured with some experimen-
tal setup. One of the strengths of our approach is that delocalization indices might
