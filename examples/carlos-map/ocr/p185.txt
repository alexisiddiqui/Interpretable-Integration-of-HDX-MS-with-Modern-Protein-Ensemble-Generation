We employ the tools of finite group theory to build a formulation of kriging that
is invariant to an arbitrary type of symmetry, which may or may not be point sym-
metry. This formulation relies heavily on the use of faithful representation matrices to
project a given configuration to a symmetric equivalent. Our derivation yields an
expression that ensures that kriging models are symmetric, but does not imply any
computational saving in principle. Fortunately, we are able to introduce a major

reduction of the points by proposing a heuristic algorithm.

Our algorithm splits the space of features in several disjoint regions equivalent
by symmetry, and employs the set of representation matrices to define mappings
between these regions. The kriging model is sampled only on one of the regions,
that is associated with the identity matrix. Whenever a prediction is done on some
features, the algorithm computes which region the point corresponds to, and maps
this point to the region that has been accurately modelled; the prediction process
proceeds as normal. This allows to incorporate symmetry to kriging models with

minor changes in the FFLUX implementation.

Although the symmetry-adapted kriging is general, to build the algorithm we
build on the concept of permutational symmetry: the invariance of a molecule (or
more rigorously, its Hamiltonian) to the permutation of atoms of the same type. For
example, permuting the labels of the hydrogen atoms in water does not alter any
property. In principle, FFLUX does not recognise permutational symmetry, since
the featurization system treats distinct atoms unequally, and the knowledge-based
nature of ordinary kriging makes it dependent on the order of the atoms. We show

that our formulation avoids these problems.

metry of our formulation. Moreover, simulation experiments on methanol and
acetaldehyde show that modelling only one of the symmetry-invariant regions is
enough to provide optimisation capabilities in all the accessible conformational
space. Particularly, we demonstrate that the optimisations recognise and avoid ro-
