Although we lack space to start a full comparison, we mention briefly that IQA
has several advantages with respect to other, more classical energy decomposition
schemes. As opposed to the celebrated Energy Decomposition Analysis (EDA) by
Morokuma [164], IQA does not require a reference state, which is desirable not only
from the point of minimality, but also when reference states can be problematic (for
example, chemical reactions). On the other hand, when compared to Natural Bond
Orbital (NBO) analysis, it can easily reflect long-range interactions that are obscured

by the localized treatment of NBO.

Unfortunately, computing some of the IQA terms is lengthy, since they require
six-dimensional integrations in an irregular boundary, that are computationally ex-
pensive due to the curse of dimensionality (see Section 3.4.3). Moreover, for a sys-
tem with N atoms, N(N — 1) such integrations will be required. Due to its elevated
computational cost, IQA is mostly employed by the theoretically-oriented commu-

nity, and has not spread as widely as other approaches.

2.4.3 Interacting Quantum Atoms and DFT

The IQA formalism is defined in terms of the first- and second-order reduced den-
sity matrices, which can be computed from the wave function. This can be done
easily for Hartree-Fock wavefunctions, and in general for any wavefunction based
on a expansion of Slater determinants (MCSCF, CI expansions, etc.). However, it is
not so clear how to do this when there is not a simple wavefunction, as in Mdller-
Plesset perturbation theory or coupled-cluster theory, or when directly there is not

a wave function, as in Density Functional Theory (DFT).

In this subsection, we explore the formulation of IQA in terms of Kohn-Sham
orbitals, which was initially presented by the Popelier group for B3LYP [165], and
then generalised by the Pendads group [166], and which will be used intensively
along this thesis. We also note that the Interacting Quantum Atoms has recently
been extended to MGller-Plesset Many-Body Perturbation Theory [167, 168], and to
Coupled-Cluster theory [169].
