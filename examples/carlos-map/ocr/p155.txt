5.2 Derivation
5.2.1 Groups and representations

Before undertaking the derivation of a symmetry-adapted version of Gaussian pro-
cess regression, two preliminary matters shall be addressed. In the first place, we
need to actually formalise the symmetries that our model will take into account; it
is simple to say that permuting the labels of methyl hydrogens does not alter the
energy, but this lacks certain generality. In the second place, it is necessary to ex-
press these symmetries in a mathematical form that allows to build an invariant

regression model.

The description of symmetry operations is best performed using the tools of
group theory, as recognised in the early days of quantum mechanics by Wigner
and von Neumann, and soon by the early quantum chemists [323]. We will follow
the approach of Longuet-Higgins, who pioneered the study of non-rigid symmetry
groups in the sixties [324]. If we consider the molecule as an aggregate of atomic

nuclei and electrons, then its Hamiltonian must be invariant under:

« Any permutation of the positions and spins of the electrons.

« Any rotation of the positions and spins of all particles (electrons and nuclei)
