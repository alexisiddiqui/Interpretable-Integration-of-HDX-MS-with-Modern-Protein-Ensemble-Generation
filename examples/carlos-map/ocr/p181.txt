5.5 Conclusions

In this chapter, we have introduced and derived symmetry-adapted kriging: a
reformulation of Gaussian process regression that allows to incorporate symmetry.
This advance is, for many reasons, an important milestone in the development of
FFLUx. In the first place, it ensures that the simulations are consistent with molec-
ular symmetry, avoiding erratic behaviour due to symmetry breakings. Even more
importantly, it allows to significantly reduce the number of evaluations during a
simulation, suggesting that larger system sizes might be accessible in the near fu-

ture.

The formulation of symmetry-adapted kriging relies in the concept of faithful
representation: a set of matrices that behave like the elements of the group. Ina
way, this set ensures that we can represent the symmetry elements in an amenable
form in terms of matrices. Employing this matrices, we propose a functional form
for the correlation functions in kriging that is invariant to symmetry, and employ

elementary finite group theory to prove this invariance.

Although this formulation, by itself, does not allow to reduce the number of
evaluations during a simulation, we introduce a clever heuristic to save compu-
tational resources. This heuristic partitions the conformational space in a set of
symmetry-equivalent regions, and introduces a procedure to evaluate to which re-
gion does a point belong to. Moreover, the matrices of the faithful representation
allow to project points in one region to points in other regions. In this way, if we
model only one of the symmetry regions, we can provide an accurate prediction in

any of the other regions, simply by projecting it into the well-modelled region.
