The radial and angular functions are respectively reminiscent of the bond length
and bond angle features of classical force fields. Both expressions are invariant
upon permutations, since a swap of coordinates simply alters the order of summa-

tion, that does not affect the result due to the commutative property of addition.

There are some related approaches that are based in employing invariant poly-
nomials (analysed below, in Section 5.1.3) to ensure that the input neurons are in-
variant to permutations [307]. This has shown good performance [308, 309], al-
though it is not as extended in the literature as the Behler-Parrinello featurization.
We note that this approach has been used widely in molecular dynamics simula-
tions of solids [310, 311, 312] and even reactions [313]. A recent extension of the
Behler-Parrinello approach in [17] has attracted significant attention in the litera-
ture.
