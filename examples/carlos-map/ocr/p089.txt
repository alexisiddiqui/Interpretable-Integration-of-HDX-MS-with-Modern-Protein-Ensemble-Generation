This potential can be better understood in the context of an example simulation,
such as the water cluster depicted in Figure 3.1. As in classical molecular mechan-
ics, there will be intramolecular and intermolecular interactions. In the first place,
every individual water molecule contributes an energy Ejga({Ra}), where {R,}
indicates that it is dependent on the total molecular configuration, and as such this

potential is reminiscent of the valence, intramolecular terms in classical force fields.

The usage of Ejgq has a number of advantages. In the first place, it makes a
direct connection with quantum mechanics, as the IQA self-energy rigorously parti-
tions the total energy. In the second place, given the non-parametric nature of atom-
istic kriging models, it might be feasible! in the following years to study processes
where bond breaking and forming is involved, competing with the few existing re-
active force fields [225]. Overall, it allows to recover the total quantum-mechanical

energy for a single molecule.
