Let us now assume that we are training a set of kriging models employing the
typical pipeline, as described in Section 3.3. If the training set is constructed by
distorting only one of the minima in Figure 5.1, there are two possible outcomes
depending on the system and the quality of the training set. In the best and most
uncommon case, the system will break down due to the extrapolation collapse (de-
scribed in Section 3.4.2), warning the user that something unexpected is going on.
In the worst case, as exemplified later in Section 5.4.2, the system will optimise to an
unexpected minimum through completely unphysical behaviour, such as ignoring

the rotation barrier.

A naive solution to this problem passes by increasing the size of the training
set, employing more than one seed. In particular, since inversion of handedness
happens only as a consequence of chemical reactions, we can build a model using
only right-handed or left-handed conformations; this is effectively employing either
the upper or lower row in Figure 5.1. Unfortunately, this results in a training set

three times as large” and has several caveats.

In the first place, it is a misuse of computational resources. Not only is it nec-
essary to compute three times as many IQA energies and multipoles, which is the
bottleneck in training, but notably, the number of evaluations during a simulation
is increased three-fold, resulting in a significant computational overhead. More im-
portantly, due to integration errors, the three minima will have different energies.
Although these differences might only be of the order of 1-2 kJ mol™!, the bias to-
wards a particular minimum can have unexpected effects in large-scale molecular

simulations.

To amend these problems without substantial changes on the previous work,
we Shall develop a framework that is able to take into account intrinsic molecular
symmetries. This methodology shall be able to recover chemical equivalences in
absence of external information, and should include only minute modifications to

the training pipeline and force field formulation.
