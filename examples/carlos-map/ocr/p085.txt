2.6 The Quantum Chemical Topology Force Field
2.6.1 Previous work

The Popelier work has been working for more than a decade in the development of
a novel polarisable force field that is able to correctly describe electrostatic interac-
tions by means of conformationally-dependent multipoles. This is done by employ-
ing Gaussian process regression to predict topological multipoles computed from
the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules. We will briefly present a historical re-
view of the most important feats in this development; a comprehensive description

of the formulation is given in Chapter 3.

The origins of this idea can be traced back to the early research in QTAIM multi-
poles described in Section 2.4.4, especially regarding the good mathematical prop-
erties of these objects. Given the fast convergence to the quantum electrostatic po-
tential, Liem and Popelier proposed a force field based on rigid molecules endowed
with static, high-order multipoles to obtain accurate electrostatics. This potential
was tested almost 15 years ago for hydrogen fluoride [199] and water [200], with
very promising results. In particular, in the former they found the spontaneous for-
mation of chains of hydrogen-bonded monomers, as well as very good agreement

of calculated bulk properties with experiment; similar results were found for water.
