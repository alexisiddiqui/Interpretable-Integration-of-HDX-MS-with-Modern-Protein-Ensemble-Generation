The definition of group is rather abstract, and there resides its power. In chem-
istry, it is usual to treat with groups containing operations of symmetry, such as
planes or rotation axes, but the notion of group is completely general. For example,
if we take the set of integers Z and set addition as the group operation, it is easy to
verify that (Z, +) verifies all the group axioms. Similarly, (IR, +) and (IR, x), where

x represents multiplication, are also groups.

As seen from these examples, groups are not limited to a finite set of elements,
as long as the group axioms are verified. The point symmetry groups for linear
molecules, Coy and Dy, are also infinite. Unfortunately, infinite groups lead to
significant mathematical difficulties, and for the rest of the text we will only treat
finite groups.
