Chapter 6

Conclusions

The current thesis presents several developments sprouted from the Quantum Chem-
ical Topology Force Field (FFLUX). This force field is a long-lasting endeavour
that aims to supersede classical molecular mechanics by introducing modern non-
parametric regression techniques that improve the valence and electrostatic poten-
tials. As comes with novel approaches, there are many open challenges that should
be addressed before the methodology is ready for deployment. The research pre-

sented here attempts to palliate these problems.

Our first development is motivated by the need of deploying inter-monomer po-
tentials for the simulation of macromolecules. In the current formulation of FFLUX
monomers interact only through electrostatics, but an exchange contribution is es-
sential to model covalent bonding, and therefore the peptide bond. Unfortunately,
it is not easy to build potentials that incorporate this magnitude: the models should
depend on the coordinates of both monomers, thus requiring a very large amount
of points. In addition, Vy: is a costly magnitude that requires the evaluation of

six-dimensional integrals, slowing down the development.

ical Topology, as a suitable alternative. In order to understand its properties and
chemical meaning, we have done a theoretical analysis and performed a careful
study on about 200 compounds, both organic and inorganic. As we proved, the
