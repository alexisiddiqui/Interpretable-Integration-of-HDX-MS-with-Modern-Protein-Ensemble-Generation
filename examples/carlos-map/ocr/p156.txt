The direct product of all the pertinent symmetry groups generated by the above
conditions forms the complete group of the Hamiltonian. In general, due to the timescales
of usual chemical phenomena, one restricts only to a fraction of such group. The

feasible elements of the complete group form the molecular symmetry group [324].

Longuet-Higgins developed a full and rich theory that allows to predict and
understand interesting properties, such as torsional spectra. However, hereafter
we will limit our scope to permutation groups: those that include permutations of the
positions’ of sets of identical nuclei. As discussed in the introduction, point groups
are a particular case of permutation groups, and it is important for the reader with

a chemical background to keep this difference in mind.

In simpler words, we can understand permutational symmetry as the fact that
the order of the coordinates in the input of a molecular simulation code is unimpor-
tant, as long as every point is assigned to the same atom type (and connectivity, in
molecular mechanics). Although this is true in general molecular simulation codes,

it is not in FFLUX, where the order of the features is important.
