The first challenge is the neglect of polarisation, which stems from the fact that
FFLUX only includes intramolecular polarisation. Two molecules of the same na-
ture will present the same charge distribution in any environment, as long as they
share the same geometry. This is an important conceptual problem, and will make
it difficult for FFLUX to compete with force fields, such as AMOEBA, that include
a physical description of polarisation. We have shown that the neglect of polari-
sation in FFLUX can yield errors of about 20 kcal mol! in energy differences for
simple water clusters, and particularly that it wrongly predicts which configuration

is more stable.

The second challenge, which we named as extrapolation collapse, represents the
problems of extrapolating beyond the training set. We provided mathematical ar-
guments that, as the predicted geometry differs significantly from the examples in
the training set, the prediction tends to a constant value, the mean of the Gaussian
process, and therefore the derivatives are null. We demonstrated the importance of
this collapse in a simple Monte Carlo simulation of a water molecule in vacuum,

and argued for the difficulties in palliating this problem.

The third challenge is a well-known problem in statistics and machine learning,
but also in numerical analysis: the curse of dimensionality. As the dimensionality of
the problem increases, even massive datasets become sparse in comparison with the
extension of the space. This results in a significant challenge when combined with
the extrapolation collapse, because it is virtually impossible to sample the whole
space for systems with more than a handful of dimensions. We demonstrate that
the curse of dimensionality is present by showing that state-of-the-art models with

~ 0.5 GB of examples fail to predict sensible energies for glycine.

Overall, all this challenges constitute open problems in the development of FFLUX.
Further work should be addressed to understand them in more detail and develop

suitable solutions.
