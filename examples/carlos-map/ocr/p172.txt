5.4.2 Simulations of realistic molecules

After validating our methodology in simple models, we implemented symmetry-
adapted kriging in DL_FFLUX as explained in Section 5.3.2. We show the effective-
ness of our approach by testing it in two case studies with one center of permuta-
tional symmetry: methanol and acetaldehyde, both having a methyl group that can
rotate freely. Note that this implies a symmetry group P(3), containing three proper
and three improper (with inversion of handedness) 120° rotations of the three hy-

drogen atoms.

Figure 5.7: Training set for methanol and acetaldehyde. The geometries were ob-
tained by distortion, employing TYCHE [211] at 3000 K and one of the symmetry-

equivalent minima as seed.

The global minima of both molecules were obtained at the B3LYP/aug-cc-pVTZ
level of theory employing GAUSSIAN 09 [235] and the verytight keyword. Training
geometries were built employing TYCHE [211], and a temperature of 3000 K; this
sampling procedure is particularly adequate because, if only one seed is employed,
it explores only one of the symmetry-equivalent configurations. The training sets
have a size of 600 points for methanol and 900 for acetaldehyde’, and are shown in

Figure 5.7.
