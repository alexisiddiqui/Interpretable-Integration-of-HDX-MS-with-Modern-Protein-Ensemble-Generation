There are a few comments to be made about this derivation. Although we have
employed the multipolar expansion in spherical tensor form to ease the notation
and derive the result rigorously, its physical meaning might be slightly obscured.
The reader should not be distracted from the fact that we are simply performing a
Taylor expansion and truncating to the first term. In fact, if one chooses to approx-
imate the denominator in Vxc(A, B) as 1/R (to be compared with equation 2.52 in

section 2.2.3), the same conclusion will follow.

In a different view, the delocalization index can be understood as the interaction
between two exchange charges. A study of the behaviour of the multipolar expansion

of the exchange interaction has been recently performed by the Pendas group [271].

Finally, we note that the delocalization index is significantly cheaper computa-
tionally that the full exchange-correlation interaction. All the integrals in equation
4.17 are 3D integrals that can be computed readily, unlike the 6D integrals in equa-
tion 4.12 that can be very burdensome. In this way, the DI is a promising quantity
that could allow to understand exchange-correlation interactions in large systems,

where calculating the full Vxc is prohibitive.
