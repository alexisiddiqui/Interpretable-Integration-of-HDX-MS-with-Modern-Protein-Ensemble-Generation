Chapter 3

The Quantum Chemical Topology
Force Field

N the previous chapter we presented an extended literature review of the
state-of-the-art in molecular simulation, with an emphasis on intermolec-
ular interactions in the context of force fields. We also provided a brief

historical review of the major feats in the development of the Quantum Chemical
Topology Force Field (QCTFF, recently renamed as FFLUX), an ambitious research
theme within our group during the last decade.

The FFLUX project has pioneered the application of machine learning to substi-
tute old force field architectures with non-parametric regression, an idea that has
had a significant impact in the literature recently [15]. However, despite the time
invested in this project (around a decade since the first developments in variable
multipoles [204]), the maximum feats have been the optimisation of water [218]
and capped glycine [219], which seem in significant delay with respect to the latest
advancements in the field [17, 18].

of becoming an useful force field. One of them is the attempt to test the kriging
methodology deeply — and thus several publications from the group [205, 206, 207,
208, 209] described repeatedly the same process of training a kriging model in dif-
