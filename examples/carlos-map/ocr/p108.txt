tained 500 training points obtained by normal mode distortion with TYCHE [211]
and computed at the B3LYP/aug-cc-pVTZ level of theory. The flat potential beyond

the training set is a clear example of the extrapolation collapse.

the energy gets stuck in a constant value, and the water molecule get distorted to

absolutely unphysical structures, such as the shown in the left side of Figure 3.6.

Eventually, the simulation ends up climbing up the potential energy barrier of
dissociation; then, since the potential is asymptotically flat as the features leave the
training set, the unphysical distortions carry a AE ~ 0, and are always accepted.
After enough time, the distortion is so strong that the system is far from any physi-

cally sensible behaviour.

We note that it is also possible to find the opposite behaviour: molecules trapped
in a flat potential because the hydrogen atoms are too close to the oxygen. This is
more rare, since the barrier is slightly higher, and there is much more feature space
after dissociation than near the collapse with the oxygen atom, but we have been

able to find it after a handful of successive runs with the same starting geometry.
