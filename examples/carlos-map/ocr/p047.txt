It is not difficult to imagine cases where the spheres of convergence can hinder
the study of intermolecular interactions, specially in moderately sized molecules. A
way to solve this problem is to divide the molecule into several regions and describe
each of these by a set of multipole moments; in this way, instead of a big divergence
sphere for the whole molecule, there are several small divergence spheres localized

in the atoms. This is displayed pictorially in Figure 2.3.

There are several methods to assign multipoles to the different regions of the
molecule. Here we present the Distributed Multipole Analysis (DMA) method by
Stone [87], although several other methods have been proposed [86, 88, 89, 90]; in
particular, in Section 2.4.4 we present an alternative based in the quantum theory

of atoms in molecules (QTAIM), that will be used in this thesis.
