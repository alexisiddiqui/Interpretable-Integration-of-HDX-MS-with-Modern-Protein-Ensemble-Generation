In Chapter 2, we provide a review of the relevant literature in the area of compu-
tational chemistry. Given the vastness of the field, the scope is limited to the topics
directly related to the research. This includes molecular dynamics, intermolecular
forces and polarisable force fields as general topics, as well as some more specific
matters: quantum chemical topology and machine learning. Finally, we also review

previous work done by the Popelier group.

In Chapter 3, we present in detail the architecture and formulation of the FFLUX
force field, including a careful derivation of all the mathematical expressions re-
quired for the calculations of atomic forces and a description of the parametriza-
tion. We also provide a critical assessment of some of the challenges that the project

will have to tackle before a significant upscaling can be possible.

In Chapter 4, we explore a magnitude from Quantum Chemical Topology, the
delocalization index. This quantity is analogous to a charge-charge interaction for
the exchange interaction, and has been analysed as a possible candidate for inter-
model interactions in FFLUX. It has also proved to play a leading role in under-

standing bond order.

In Chapter 5, we develop a new technique: symmetry-adapted Gaussian Pro-
cess regression. This method can be used to incorporate permutational symmetry
into the models, and allows to reduce the size of the training set. We study this ap-
proach in a number of simple molecules, and show how it can be used to optimise

geometries with particularly reduced training sizes.

Finally, in Chapter 6 we gather the main conclusions of this work, and outline a

set of future lines of work that might be taken by future students of the group.
