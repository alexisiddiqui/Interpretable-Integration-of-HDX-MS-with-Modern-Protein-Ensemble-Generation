It is not difficult to come up with simple examples that challenge this interpre-
tation. For example, in a protein, the environment of a residue will be considerably
different depending on whether it is placed in the hydrophobic core of the pep-
tide, or in the surface, exposed to the solvent [103, 104]. Similarly, it is likely that
the constraint imposed by the rest of the residues limits the flexibility of the sin-
gle aminoacid, affecting the intramolecular polarisation. The approach in FFLUX
might find difficult to model these systems, especially in comparison with success-

ful formulations such as AMOEBA that include polarisation explicitly.

We propose the following simple experiment, as an initial test of the validity
of the approximation. We will select a system of several molecules, and compare
the electrostatic energy as calculated with its permanent atomic multipoles only (i.
e. the multipoles computed for each of its constituent molecules, in vacuum), and
with the real multipoles, that include the polarisation components (i. e. the atomic
multipoles computed for each atom in the total system). This emulates the process
that occurs during a simulation, where the atomic multipoles of each molecule are
obtained by interpolation of in vacuo values, and avoids other possible sources of

error.

Given the importance of polarisation in water, and the ubiquity of this solvent in
biological systems, we will employ as test system a cluster of five water molecules.
To obtain several conformations, we built an arbitrary system using Avogadro [239],
pre-optimised it using MMFF94 [240], and ran 5 ps BP86/Def2SVP BOMD simula-
tions at 600 K employing ORCA [241] and the Berendsen thermostat. Obviating
equilibration, five snapshots were sampled randomly, and their geometries were
optimised at the RI-MP2/cc-pVTZ level of theory. Note that the only objective of
the BOMD simulation is to distort the system and obtain different conformations,
so the difference in methods and basis sets is unimportant. Finally, single point
MP2/cc-pVTZ calculations on the optimal cluster geometries were performed with
GAUSSIAN 09 [235].
