The delocalization index is defined in terms of the second-order reduced density
matrix (or more rigorously its diagonal part, the pair density). Given the complexity
of an arbitrary correlated 2-RDM, a key step in our research was to validate Miiller
approximation: a Hartree-Fock-like estimate of the 2-RDM in terms of the 1-RDM.
Our benchmark showed that this approximation allows to compute delocalization
indices within a 10% error bound for directly bonded atoms, although larger errors

can be obtained when correlation is essential.

A large scale statistical analysis verified a fact suspected by the QCT community,
but never explored in detail: that the delocalization index is a bond order indicator.
We have shown that delocalization indices vary in a quasi-continuous fashion, but
cluster around certain well-defined values that can be identified with the classical
bond orders present in Lewis diagrams. We have also shown that there is a sharp
discontinuity between bonded and non-bonded terms, thus providing another evi-

dence of chemical bonding.

Finally, we attempted to extend the applicability of this approach by exploring
several exotic compounds. These included a Ng molecule, a compound with pla-
nar tetracoordinate carbon and a metallic complex whose actual bonding pattern
has been object of discussion in the literature. In all cases, we obtained sensible an-
swers, sometimes superseding other approaches in the literature. Specifically, we
discarded the presence of an ionic bond in Ng, that was suggested by NBO, and
provided a description of the metallic complex that is coherent with both hapticity
and electron-counting techniques.
