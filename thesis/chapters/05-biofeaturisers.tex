\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:biofeat-intro}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:biofeat-motivation}

\Cref{ch:valdx,ch:jaxent} assessed and proposed conformational ensembles by reweighting and optimising fixed candidates after sampling had finished. This offline integration redistributes weight among the structures supplied but cannot add a state they lack, and each change of candidate ensemble costs another round of sampling and fitting. To address sampling efficiently, we need the data to be integrated, and the agreement assessed, online while conformations are being simulated or generated.

For restrained simulation, metainference with metadynamics is a common approach to this problem. Metainference restrains replica averages to the data and infers their uncertainty during sampling, while metadynamics carries the replicas over barriers that the restraint alone does not cross.\supercite{bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016} It has been applied to SAXS,\supercite{ahmedRefinementASynucleinEnsembles2021} but no widely used implementation exists for HDX. The BV model counts contacts with hard cutoffs, so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates and gives no force to follow (\cref{eq:bg-bv-feature}), and PLUMED provides no optimised HDX collective variable. Python sampling libraries do not close this gap. PySAGES runs enhanced-sampling methods on GPUs through JAX,\supercite{zubietaricoPySAGESFlexibleAdvanced2024} but it does not provide hooks for parallel-bias metadynamics as implemented in PLUMED,\supercite{pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015} which biases several collective variables at once.

Generation with modern protein structure models poses the same problem with a different sampler. Diffusion generators propose candidates without the cost of simulation, but generated samples collapse onto the training distribution and do not approximate the solution ensemble (\cref{sec:bg-diffusion}). Experimental guidance can enter during denoising, although guiding each structure towards an ensemble-average target such as HDX uptake can exclude the conformations whose combination would explain it (\cref{sec:bg-guided-diffusion}). We therefore carry these concepts from metainference with metadynamics into the diffusion generation process.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:biofeat-methods}

\subsection{Shared Forward-Model Interface}
\label{sec:biofeat-interface}

One forward-model definition serves reweighting, restrained simulation and guided generation, because each needs the same chain from coordinates to observables to mismatch and back to derivatives. BioFeaturisers computes the observables (residue-wise $\ln P_f$, peptide uptake or a scattering profile) from coordinates in JAX\supercite{bradburyJAXComposableTransformations2018} and returns vector--Jacobian products, so each consumer passes the gradient of its own mismatch with respect to the observables and receives the gradient with respect to its own variables. jaxENT supplies frame weights and forward-model parameters over fixed structures (\cref{ch:jaxent}). PLUMED\supercite{bonomiPromotingTransparencyReproducibility2019} supplies coordinates at each MD step and receives an atomic force.
A PyTorch generator supplies coordinates during denoising and receives a coordinate gradient through a custom autograd function. % cite: PyTorch
The forward model is therefore written and tested once, and the applications differ only in what they let the data change.

\subsection{Smooth-Contact BV Model}
\label{sec:biofeat-bv}

Returning coordinate derivatives requires the forward model to vary smoothly with atom positions. The BV model instead counts heavy-atom contacts and hydrogen bonds with hard distance cutoffs (\cref{eq:bg-bv-feature}),\supercite{bestStructuralInterpretationHydrogen2006} so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates. BioFeaturisers replaces each step with a sigmoid,
\begin{equation}
    s(d;r,b)=\frac{1}{1+\exp[-b(r-d)]},
    \label{eq:biofeat-switch}
\end{equation}
as in Wan et al.,\supercite{wanReconcilingSimulatedEnsembles2020} so predicted protection varies smoothly with every atom position, and a steep sigmoid recovers the hard cutoff. At a steepness of $5\,\text{\AA}^{-1}$ a contact switches over about $0.9\,\text{\AA}$ around its cutoff, so atoms near a cutoff carry fractional counts, and $\beta$ coefficients calibrated with hard cutoffs do not transfer automatically. Pairs on the same chain within $m$ residues of the probe are excluded from both counts, and $m$ is a modelling choice that the BPTI case varies.

To predict ensemble uptake, protection is first combined across structures in $\ln P_f$ (\cref{eq:bg-bv-average}), before exchange is applied. Peptide uptake is therefore that of the geometric-mean protection and differs from the mean of per-frame uptakes wherever protection varies between frames. The contact sums, parameter defaults and case-specific settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-bv}.

\subsection{SAXS Forward Model}
\label{sec:biofeat-saxs}

The same interface carries a SAXS profile, which constrains overall size and shape where HDX constrains residue environments. The profile is the Debye sum with FoXS form factors for the displaced solvent and the hydration layer,\supercite{debyeZerstreuungRontgenstrahlen1915,schneidman-duhovnyFoXSWebServer2010} evaluated directly over atom pairs or on a grid by Fourier transform for large structures.\supercite{chamberlainFittingHighresolutionElectron2023} No case in this chapter applies a SAXS restraint. The form factors, solvent terms and both numerical implementations are given in \cref{app:biofeat-saxs}.

\subsection{Restrained and Enhanced Sampling}
\label{sec:biofeat-metainference}
\label{sec:biofeat-metadynamics}

To use ensemble HDX predictions during simulation, we coupled the forward model to metainference through the shared interface (\cref{sec:biofeat-interface}). Metainference restrains the average over replicas and infers an uncertainty for each observation alongside the coordinates,\supercite{bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016} so the data act at the ensemble level at which they were measured and inconsistent observations are down-weighted by the sampling itself. The force path and simulation settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-simulation}, and the uncertainty bounds in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}.

A restraint changes which structures are favoured but does not carry replicas over barriers, so exploration needs an additional bias. Parallel-bias metadynamics builds a one-dimensional history-dependent bias on each of several collective variables and combines them with conditional weights for their coupling, with walkers sharing one history.\supercite{pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015} In metainference with parallel-bias metadynamics (MI+PB) the total bias enters metainference as a reweighting term,\supercite{alamdariEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2021} which corrects the replica average that the restraint compares with the data. This correction does not turn pooled frames from a time-dependent bias into equilibrium populations, so restrained ensembles here supply coverage and candidates, and populations come from unbiased dynamics or reweighting. Bias settings are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}.

\subsection{Metadiffusion}
\label{sec:biofeat-metadiffusion}

In diffusion generation, we likewise separated exploration of collective motion from refinement against HDX. In TeaA, HDX guidance applied at every denoising step of Boltz-2 left the ensemble at the Boltz intermediate because the per-atom gradient was incoherent across atoms. % cite: Boltz-2
We therefore used a collective bias to expand inter-lobe motion before refining structures against HDX. The lobe division comes from the Boltz pair representation, which groups strongly coupled residues, and the hinge axis from the relative lobe rotations in an unguided ensemble. The resulting angle $\theta$ measures rotation about this axis without an MD reference or a hand-chosen lobe boundary (\cref{eq:biofeat-theta}).

To expand coverage of $\theta$, a history-dependent Gaussian bias displaces structures away from previously visited angles, with new deposits reduced where the bias is already high.\supercite{barducciWelltemperedMetadynamicsSmoothly2008} Biasing acts in a short window late in denoising, because earlier displacements are undone by subsequent denoising and the hinge ceases to move at lower noise. HDX guidance is switched off in this window. Continuation rounds re-noise previous structures into the window and retain the bias history, extending the sampled range. The displacement rule and bias are specified in \cref{eq:biofeat-metad-bias,eq:biofeat-metad-move}, with window and cap diagnostics in \cref{app:biofeat-metadiffusion-diagnostics}.

Hinge rotation alone does not determine the local packing measured by the HDX forward model. We therefore selected structures across the sampled angles and re-denoised them with ensemble-level HDX guidance. At each guided step, the structures' $\ln P_f$ values are averaged under the current MaxEnt weights and converted to uptake. The squared uptake mismatch is differentiated with respect to every structure's coordinates, so each moves according to its contribution to the ensemble error. MaxEnt refits alternate with coordinate refinement (\cref{sec:bg-maxent,ch:jaxent}), and the guidance is scaled to the Boltz update to limit structural distortion. Refinement and fitting settings are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-metadiffusion}.

Guided samples are not equilibrium-weighted (\cref{sec:bg-diffusion}), so state populations were estimated by a final MaxEnt fit. After refinement, structures below pLDDT 0.7 were removed and MaxEnt was fitted over the retained pool with a uniform prior. Summing weights over ranges of $\theta$ gives the state populations. The sampling bias is never read as a free energy.

\subsection{Case-Study Designs}
\label{sec:biofeat-cases}

BPTI tests recovery of reference basins, MoPrP tests the response to a shared HDX restraint under different protonation states, and TeaA tests recovery of known populations from guided generation. The collective variables (CVs) in \cref{tab:biofeat-cvs} target disulfide rearrangement in BPTI, local unfolding and segment displacement in MoPrP, and inter-lobe rotation in TeaA. The Gaussian widths and wall settings for MD are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}, and the TeaA bias settings in \cref{tab:biofeat-metadiffusion}.

\paragraph{BPTI.}
BPTI asks whether restrained sampling recovers the basins of a long reference simulation from a small fraction of its simulated time. The restraints used 41 residue-level HDX-NMR protection factors, and the 1.03\,ms Anton trajectory\supercite{shawAtomiclevelCharacterizationStructural2010} supplied the structural reference and none of the targets. The design separates generating diverse candidates from sampling with the correct exclusions. MI+PB with $\pm0$ exclusions, which remove only the probe residue from contact counting, generated the source ensemble, biased on the $\chi_3$ dihedrals of the three disulfides. Production used $\pm2$ exclusions, which also remove the two residues on either side.

Four structures selected across the source ensemble, one per replica, seeded three matched conditions from identical coordinates. These were $\pm2$ MI+PB, $\pm2$ MI and unbiased MD, the last testing what the generated diversity retains under the force field alone. Each stage ran four 250 ns replicas, so a route consisting of the diversity stage and one $\pm2$ condition totals 2\,$\mu$s of production, 500-fold less simulated time than the Anton reference. All ensembles were projected without refitting onto the Anton C$\alpha$ PCA, and occupancy of the second basin was followed pooled, per replica and in 10 ns windows. These densities come from uniform frame counts, so they measure coverage and retention, not populations. Start selection and projection details are given in \cref{app:biofeat-bpti}.

\paragraph{MoPrP.}
MoPrP compares pH 4 with pH 7 under one experimental restraint. Both systems were built from the same AlphaFold3 model of mouse PrP 122--230\supercite{abramsonAccurateStructurePrediction2024} with protonation fixed at each nominal pH by PDB2PQR and PROPKA.\supercite{dolinskyPDB2PQRExpandingUpgrading2007,olssonPROPKA3ConsistentTreatment2011} Both were restrained by the same 77 residue-wise $\ln P_f$ values, recovered by exPfact from the HDX-MS data of Moulick et al.\supercite{skinnerEstimatingConstraintsProtection2019,moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} The paired simulations therefore compare how the force field at each protonation responds to one restraint.

MI+PB at each pH generated candidates, with the bias on five collective variables placed on the features of the partially unfolded forms (PUFs). These are the $\beta1$--$\beta2$ sheet contacts, the displacements of $\beta1$ and of the $\alpha1$--$\beta2$ unit, and the helicity of $\alpha1$ and of the C-terminal end of $\alpha3$. To follow how these states persist and connect, unbiased trajectories were seeded from three basins of each MI+PB ensemble on a Cartesian C$\alpha$ PCA fitted to the 20 NMR models of PDB 2L39. Twelve seeds per basin were each run at both target pH values, so every seed starts from the same frame at pH 4 and pH 7, for 12.48\,$\mu$s of production in total. Preparation, restrained sampling and the paired seed protocol are given in \cref{app:biofeat-moprp}.

\paragraph{TeaA.}
TeaA asks whether metadiffusion recovers known populations with no MD structure in any run. The target is synthetic HDX uptake from MD frames at 40\,\% open and 60\,\% closed, made with the same forward model that guides the generation, so the comparison carries no forward-model error. Generation saw the sequence, Boltz-2's own MSA-conditioned prediction as its only template, and the uptake. The MD open and closed structures entered only the scoring.

An unguided control from the same seed structures was reweighted in the same way, so the gain from guidance is measured against the generator's own coverage. States come from $\theta$ (open below $-5^\circ$, closed above $+5^\circ$, otherwise intermediate), and recovery is $100(1-\sqrt{\mathrm{JSD}})$ over the three states as in \cref{ch:jaxent}. Both are evaluation measures and never enter a loss. How sharply uptake agreement distinguishes open fractions sets how closely the recovered populations can be read. To measure it, mixtures of open and closed structures close to the MD references were scored against the target with the same forward model, at open fractions from 0 to 1. Target construction and control settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-teaa-evaluation}, and reference checks in \cref{app:biofeat-teaa-checks}.

\subsection{MoPrP State Assignment and Dynamics}
\label{sec:biofeat-moprp-analysis}

The PUF descriptions combine local change in $\beta1$ with displacement of whole segments, so the MoPrP trajectories were followed on two planes. The PCA plane places frames by global Cartesian displacement from the NMR models, the projection on which the seeds were selected. The dihedral plane used for MoPrP in \cref{ch:jaxent} plots the Ramachandran dihedral RMSD to 2L39 (dRMSD) against the dihedral deviation of $\beta1$, which separates local change in $\beta1$ from global disorder.

These projections describe global and local conformational change, while the experimental PUF1 and PUF2 descriptions identify which segments lose protection.\supercite{moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} \Cref{tab:biofeat-puf-definitions} distinguishes these descriptions from the rules used to identify candidates in simulated frames. To test those descriptions in simulated frames, we measured how far each segment moved relative to the core. Each frame was superposed on the disulfide-proximal core, and a segment counted as displaced when its core-fit RMSD exceeded the 99th percentile of Folded frames. The RMSD after superposing the segment on itself distinguishes rigid displacement from internal disorder. A segment that moves as a rigid body keeps a low self-fit RMSD. Threshold calibration and segment definitions are given in \cref{app:biofeat-moprp-assignment,tab:biofeat-moprp-segments}.

Segment rules test each PUF signature independently and can overlap (\cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-rules}). For analyses requiring one assignment per frame, structural clusters resolve overlaps in a fixed priority order (\cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-clusters}). The PUF2 cluster additionally requires $\beta1$ displacement and takes precedence over PUF1; Folded-like is the residual class. These assignments are distinct from the landscape regions and historical seed labels, whose definitions are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-regions,tab:biofeat-moprp-basins}.

Seeded runs start away from equilibrium, so their frame counts reflect where they began. Markov state models (MSMs) estimate stationary populations from the transitions the runs make.\supercite{prinzMarkovModelsMolecular2011} The MSM used 100 microstates from $k$-means on 10 Cartesian PCs fitted to unbiased MD, with a reversible maximum-likelihood fit at a lag of 5 ns. Not every macrostate passed the Chapman--Kolmogorov test, so we used the MSM to estimate stationary populations and did not interpret its transition rates (\cref{app:biofeat-moprp-msm}). Opening free energies follow the EX2 relation from the stationary probabilities of open and closed states (\cref{eq:bg-opening-free-energy}), which places them on the scale of the experimental values.

To describe the direction of motion separately from stationary populations, drift was measured directly as the mean displacement per nanosecond on each projection (\cref{app:biofeat-moprp-drift}). Drift describes motion in the projection, not in the full coordinates. Because every seed ran at both pH values, the pH 7 minus pH 4 change in drift compares the same 72 seeds.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:biofeat-results}

\subsection{Restrained Sampling from Diverse Starts Retains Both Anton Basins}
\label{sec:biofeat-bpti-results}

Experimental restraints can improve agreement with HDX without producing an ensemble that covers the relevant conformational states. To assess whether using the forward model during sampling supplies useful structural diversity, we compared short restrained simulations of BPTI with the 1.03\,ms Anton trajectory. BPTI provides experimental HDX-NMR protection factors for the restraints and a separate structural reference against which to assess the sampled conformations. We asked whether the short simulations covered both principal Anton basins, and whether that coverage persisted when the sampling bias or the experimental restraint was removed. Anton supplied no restraint targets.

Biasing the disulfide sidechain torsions did not by itself produce the backbone displacement needed to reach the left Anton basin. With $\pm2$ residue exclusions, MI+PB from the original starts remained concentrated in the main basin. We therefore introduced an additional perturbation through the HDX restraint, reducing the exclusions to $\pm0$ so that neighbouring residues contributed to the contact counts. This changed the coordinate-dependent restraint while retaining the same sidechain CVs. Under this condition, the backbone reached the left basin, defined by PC1 $<-0.55$, which contained 48.0\,\% of the source frames (\cref{fig:biofeat-bpti-matched-starts}).

The $\pm0$ stage supplied the additional displacement needed to generate diverse starts, but left unresolved whether the resulting backbone conformations would persist under the production $\pm2$ model. We therefore selected four structures across the source ensemble and restarted them under matched $\pm2$ MI+PB, $\pm2$ MI and unbiased conditions. These comparisons test whether retaining the left basin requires continued torsional bias, the HDX restraint alone, or neither.

Both restrained conditions retained coverage of the two Anton basins after restart, with similar left-basin frame fractions under MI alone and MI+PB. Pooled over the four replicas, 44.5\,\% of MI+PB frames and 44.9\,\% of MI frames lay in the left basin, compared with 35.6\,\% for unbiased MD from identical starts. Over the final 50\,ns, these fractions were 37.0, 39.2 and 22.4\,\%, respectively. Both restrained conditions also retained greater left-basin coverage than unbiased MD when the boundary was moved to PC1 $=-0.65$ or $-0.45$ (\cref{app:biofeat-bpti}). Continued disulfide bias was therefore not required to retain this coverage over the matched 250\,ns restarts.

The HDX restraint prolonged retention of the left basin after the initial perturbation (\cref{fig:biofeat-bpti-replica-retention}). Of the two replicas starting there, one remained predominantly in the basin under every condition. The other left after 151.5\,ns in unbiased MD, compared with 200.4\,ns under MI+PB and 217.3\,ns under MI, and subsequently returned under MI+PB. The additional perturbation therefore provided access to the left basin, while the production HDX restraint helped retain it.

The diversity stage followed by one matched production condition covered both Anton basins using 2\,$\mu$s of aggregate production simulation, approximately 500-fold less simulated time than the reference. These frame counts establish coverage and retention; estimating populations requires accounting for the selected starts and the sampling bias. We next used restrained sampling to supply candidate states for MoPrP, then followed their dynamics after removing the restraints.

\subsection{Partial Opening Restricts Return towards the Native MoPrP Fold}
\label{sec:biofeat-moprp-results}

Moulick et al.'s native-state HDX experiments identified at least two partially unfolded forms of MoPrP under misfolding-promoting conditions at pH 4 (\cref{fig:intro-prion-populations}).\supercite{moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} The denaturant dependence of exchange grouped residues into subglobal opening events, providing segment-level descriptions and opening free energies for PUF1 and PUF2. These measurements establish which regions become exposed together, but leave their structural arrangements unresolved. We used HDX-restrained enhanced sampling to propose conformations underlying these openings, then examined whether they persisted and how they returned towards the native fold after release.

Mutational studies connect this partial opening to specific interactions stabilising MoPrP. H186F stabilises the protein by 1.75\,kcal/mol at pH 4 but has little effect at pH 7, implicating H186 protonation in acidic destabilisation. Neutralising either partner of the R155--D201 salt bridge also destabilises MoPrP and accelerates misfolding, with the pathogenic D201N substitution mimicking neutralisation of D201. HDX-MS places the increased dynamics around $\alpha1$ and the $\alpha1$--$\beta2$ loop.\supercite{singhUnravelingMolecularMechanism2016} These results motivate examining how protonation changes the packing of these elements against the core, and whether their displacement accompanies $\beta1$ detachment.

Short constant-pH simulations already provide a precedent for protonation-dependent access to alternative PrP conformations. Campos et al. simulated human PrP across pH 2--7 in 30--40\,ns trajectories and associated protonation of His187, corresponding to mouse H186, with loss of interactions between the $\beta1$--$\alpha1$--$\beta2$ and $\alpha2$--$\alpha3$ subdomains. One trajectory at pH 2 formed a stable $\beta$-rich structure.\supercite{camposConstantpHMolecularDynamics2010} This coupling between protonation and structural rearrangement supports pH as a possible gating potential. Later MoPrP experiments showed that folding and unfolding follow the same pathway at pH 7 but distinct routes at pH 4, where the kinetics also depend on initial conditions.\supercite{moulickRuggednessFreeEnergy2019} The landscape interpretation in \cref{fig:intro-frustrated-memory} therefore asks how solution conditions alter access between conformations and how a reached arrangement can retain the history of the perturbation applied.

We examined this structural basis by generating partially opened conformations under HDX restraint at pH 4 and pH 7, then following their persistence, exchange and return without restraints. Both restrained simulations used the same experimental protection-factor target, so the comparison tested how the two fixed-protonation models responded to one restraint. Structures selected across their sampled basins subsequently supplied paired starts for unbiased simulations at both pH values. The representative structures identify which experimental opening units rearranged, while the paired trajectories test whether changing protonation alters their subsequent dynamics.

Restrained sampling at pH 4 and pH 7 produced distinct ways of disrupting native packing (\cref{fig:biofeat-moprp-basins}). At pH 4, the ensemble extended along an arm dominated by $\beta1$ detachment. At pH 7, it also reached a core-distorted lobe and a detached $\beta1$--$\alpha1$ set, where $\beta1$, the intervening loop and the N-terminal part of $\alpha1$ moved away from the core. The representative structures distinguish separation of native elements from deformation within them. In the detached set, $\alpha1$ retained a self-fit RMSD of 0.02--0.04\,nm while moving away from the core. Its displacement therefore represents loss of native packing rather than internal unfolding.

Removing the restraints revealed different subsequent dynamics for Folded and partially opened starts (\cref{fig:biofeat-moprp-paths}). Folded starts remained confined near the native region, with no frame crossing the $\beta1$-opening threshold. The core-distorted starts explored a branch connected to Folded, while the detached $\beta1$--$\alpha1$ starts predominantly retained $\beta1$ opening and exchanged with arrangements along the arm. Direct return from the detached set to Folded was rare. Partial opening therefore changed the conformations accessible over the sampled durations. This provides a structural basis for the history-dependence, or potential-gated dynamics introduced in \cref{fig:intro-frustrated-memory}.

The experimental opening free energies provide a second test of the sampled arrangements. We estimated stationary populations from the unbiased trajectories and compared the resulting candidate opening free energies with the HDX values (\cref{fig:biofeat-moprp-msm}). At pH 4, the closest PUF1 and PUF2 candidates had opening free energies of 2.60 and 3.35\,kcal/mol, compared with $2.2\pm0.2$ and $3.1\pm0.5$\,kcal/mol experimentally.\supercite{moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} Both candidates occurred within the detached $\beta1$--$\alpha1$ set. Their structural correspondence was incomplete. No sampled frame combined $\beta1$ detachment with the C-terminal $\alpha3$ disorder described for PUF1, and the PUF2 candidate shifted $\beta2$ without detaching it. The energetic comparison places these candidates near the experimental opening costs, while the segment-level comparison identifies the structural changes still missing.

Protonation changed the estimated distribution among the sampled arrangements. Core distortion was 2.3--2.8 times more populated at pH 4, and displacement of the $\alpha1$--$\beta2$ unit was 7--10 times more populated, across the two MSM lag times. At pH 7, the PCA map instead contained a local minimum along the $\beta1$-detached arm, marked by the star in \cref{fig:biofeat-moprp-msm}. These differences describe relative favourability within the sampled model. Whether protonation also changes the routes of return requires examining motion through these regions.

Local motion differed around the occupied regions (\cref{fig:biofeat-moprp-flow}). At pH 7, a slow corridor extended from the Folded region towards the arm minimum. At pH 4, the slow region branched towards both the core-distorted lobe and the detached arm. The dihedral projection separated changes in $\beta1$ from displacement elsewhere in the protein. These patterns locate where return or further rearrangement slows within the sampled ensemble, connecting the population maps to the selective exchange seen in the individual trajectories.

These simulations provide the potential-gated landscape interpretation of MoPrP dynamics with specific structural content. Detachment of $\beta1$ and displacement of the intact $\alpha1$ helix produce persistent arrangements with restricted routes of return, while Folded starts do not reach those arrangements over the sampled durations. By combining restrained and unbiased simulations under multiple pH conditions, we identified candidate conformations for the partially unfolded forms and characterise a structural basis for the history-dependence of MoPrP dynamics. 

While these simulation experiments sample an equilibrium ensemble landscape larger than previous studies, fixed topologies mean these simulations do not represent the full equilibrium ensemble of MoPrP in solution. These results exemplify how we can incorporate multiple sources of information to generate and refine ensembles, and how we can use the resulting ensembles to extrapolate structural explanations for observations, beyond what could be achieved by any single experiment in isolation.
