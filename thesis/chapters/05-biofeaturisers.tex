\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:biofeat-intro}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:biofeat-motivation}

\Cref{ch:valdx,ch:jaxent} assessed and proposed conformational ensembles by reweighting and optimising fixed candidates after sampling had finished. This offline integration redistributes weight among the structures supplied but cannot add a state they lack, and each change of candidate ensemble costs another round of sampling and fitting. To address sampling efficiently, we need the data to be integrated, and the agreement assessed, online while conformations are being simulated or generated.

For restrained simulation, metainference with metadynamics is a common approach to this problem. Metainference restrains replica averages to the data and infers their uncertainty during sampling, while metadynamics carries the replicas over barriers that the restraint alone does not cross.\supercite{bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016} It has been applied to SAXS,\supercite{ahmedRefinementASynucleinEnsembles2021} but no widely used implementation exists for HDX. The BV model counts contacts with hard cutoffs, so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates and gives no force to follow (\cref{eq:bg-bv-feature}), and PLUMED provides no optimised HDX collective variable. Python sampling libraries do not close this gap. PySAGES runs enhanced-sampling methods on GPUs through JAX,\supercite{zubietaricoPySAGESFlexibleAdvanced2024} but it does not provide hooks for parallel-bias metadynamics as implemented in PLUMED,\supercite{pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015} which biases several collective variables at once.

Generation with modern protein structure models poses the same problem with a different sampler. Diffusion generators propose candidates without the cost of simulation, but generated samples collapse onto the training distribution and do not approximate the solution ensemble (\cref{sec:bg-diffusion}). Experimental guidance can enter during denoising, although guiding each structure towards an ensemble-average target such as HDX uptake can exclude the conformations whose combination would explain it (\cref{sec:bg-guided-diffusion}). We therefore carry these concepts from metainference with metadynamics into the diffusion generation process.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:biofeat-methods}

\subsection{Shared Forward-Model Interface}
\label{sec:biofeat-interface}

One forward-model definition serves reweighting, restrained simulation and guided generation, because each needs the same chain from coordinates to observables to mismatch and back to derivatives. BioFeaturisers computes the observables (residue-wise $\ln P_f$, peptide uptake or a scattering profile) from coordinates in JAX\supercite{bradburyJAXComposableTransformations2018} and returns vector--Jacobian products, so each consumer passes the gradient of its own mismatch with respect to the observables and receives the gradient with respect to its own variables. jaxENT supplies frame weights and forward-model parameters over fixed structures (\cref{ch:jaxent}). PLUMED\supercite{bonomiPromotingTransparencyReproducibility2019} supplies coordinates at each MD step and receives an atomic force.
A PyTorch generator supplies coordinates during denoising and receives a coordinate gradient through a custom autograd function. % cite: PyTorch
The forward model is therefore written and tested once, and the applications differ only in what they let the data change.

\subsection{Smooth-Contact BV Model}
\label{sec:biofeat-bv}

The BV model counts heavy-atom contacts and hydrogen bonds with hard distance cutoffs (\cref{eq:bg-bv-feature}),\supercite{bestStructuralInterpretationHydrogen2006} so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates and has no gradient to follow. BioFeaturisers replaces each step with a sigmoid,
\begin{equation}
    s(d;r,b)=\frac{1}{1+\exp[-b(r-d)]},
    \label{eq:biofeat-switch}
\end{equation}
as in Wan et al.,\supercite{wanReconcilingSimulatedEnsembles2020} so predicted protection varies smoothly with every atom position, and a steep sigmoid recovers the hard cutoff. At a steepness of $5\,\text{\AA}^{-1}$ a contact switches over about $0.9\,\text{\AA}$ around its cutoff, so atoms near a cutoff carry fractional counts, and $\beta$ coefficients calibrated with hard cutoffs do not transfer automatically. Pairs on the same chain within $m$ residues of the probe are excluded from both counts, and $m$ is a modelling choice that the BPTI case varies.

Predictions are combined across structures in $\ln P_f$ (\cref{eq:bg-bv-average}), before exchange is applied. The contact sums, parameter defaults and case-specific settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-bv}. Peptide uptake is therefore that of the geometric-mean protection, and it differs from the mean of per-frame uptakes wherever protection varies between frames.

\subsection{SAXS Forward Model}
\label{sec:biofeat-saxs}

The same interface carries a SAXS profile, which constrains overall size and shape where HDX constrains residue environments. The profile is the Debye sum with FoXS form factors for the displaced solvent and the hydration layer,\supercite{debyeZerstreuungRontgenstrahlen1915,schneidman-duhovnyFoXSWebServer2010} evaluated directly over atom pairs or on a grid by Fourier transform for large structures.\supercite{chamberlainFittingHighresolutionElectron2023} No case in this chapter applies a SAXS restraint. The form factors, solvent terms and both numerical implementations are given in \cref{app:biofeat-saxs}.

\subsection{Restrained and Enhanced Sampling}
\label{sec:biofeat-metainference}
\label{sec:biofeat-metadynamics}

Metainference restrains the average over replicas and infers an uncertainty for each observation alongside the coordinates,\supercite{bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016} so the data act at the ensemble level at which they were measured and inconsistent observations are down-weighted by the sampling itself. BioFeaturisers couples this restraint to the coordinates through the shared interface (\cref{sec:biofeat-interface}). The force path and simulation settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-simulation}, and the uncertainty bounds in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}.

A restraint changes which structures are favoured but does not carry replicas over barriers, so exploration needs an additional bias. Parallel-bias metadynamics builds a one-dimensional history-dependent bias on each of several collective variables and combines them with conditional weights for their coupling, with walkers sharing one history.\supercite{pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015} In metainference with parallel-bias metadynamics (MI+PB) the total bias enters metainference as a reweighting term,\supercite{alamdariEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2021} which corrects the replica average that the restraint compares with the data. Bias settings are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}. This correction does not turn pooled frames from a time-dependent bias into equilibrium populations, so restrained ensembles here supply coverage and candidates, and populations come from unbiased dynamics or reweighting.

\subsection{Metadiffusion}
\label{sec:biofeat-metadiffusion}

In TeaA, HDX guidance applied at every denoising step of Boltz-2 left the ensemble at the Boltz intermediate because the per-atom gradient was incoherent across atoms. % cite: Boltz-2
We therefore used a collective bias to expand inter-lobe motion before refining structures against HDX. The lobe division comes from the Boltz pair representation, which groups strongly coupled residues, and the hinge axis from the relative lobe rotations in an unguided ensemble. The resulting angle $\theta$ measures rotation about this axis without an MD reference or a hand-chosen lobe boundary (\cref{eq:biofeat-theta}).

To expand coverage of $\theta$, a history-dependent Gaussian bias displaces structures away from previously visited angles, with new deposits reduced where the bias is already high.\supercite{barducciWelltemperedMetadynamicsSmoothly2008} Biasing acts in a short window late in denoising, because earlier displacements are undone by subsequent denoising and the hinge ceases to move at lower noise. HDX guidance is switched off in this window. Continuation rounds re-noise previous structures into the window and retain the bias history, extending the sampled range. The displacement rule and bias are specified in \cref{eq:biofeat-metad-bias,eq:biofeat-metad-move}, with window and cap diagnostics in \cref{app:biofeat-metadiffusion-diagnostics}.

Hinge rotation alone does not determine the local packing measured by the HDX forward model. We therefore selected structures across the sampled angles and re-denoised them with ensemble-level HDX guidance. At each guided step, the structures' $\ln P_f$ values are averaged under the current MaxEnt weights and converted to uptake. The squared uptake mismatch is differentiated with respect to every structure's coordinates, so each moves according to its contribution to the ensemble error. MaxEnt refits alternate with coordinate refinement (\cref{sec:bg-maxent,ch:jaxent}), and the guidance is scaled to the Boltz update to limit structural distortion. Refinement and fitting settings are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-metadiffusion}.

After refinement, structures below pLDDT 0.7 are removed and MaxEnt is fitted over the retained pool with a uniform prior. Summing weights over ranges of $\theta$ gives the state populations. Guided samples are not equilibrium-weighted (\cref{sec:bg-diffusion}), so populations come from this final fit and the sampling bias is never read as a free energy.

\subsection{Case-Study Designs}
\label{sec:biofeat-cases}

The collective variables (CVs) in \cref{tab:biofeat-cvs} target disulfide rearrangement in BPTI, local unfolding and segment displacement in MoPrP, and inter-lobe rotation in TeaA. The Gaussian widths and wall settings for MD are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-mipb}, and the TeaA bias settings in \cref{tab:biofeat-metadiffusion}.

\paragraph{BPTI.}
BPTI asks whether restrained sampling recovers the basins of a long reference simulation from a small fraction of its simulated time. The restraints used 41 residue-level HDX-NMR protection factors, and the 1.03\,ms Anton trajectory\supercite{shawAtomiclevelCharacterizationStructural2010} supplied the structural reference and none of the targets. The design separates generating diverse candidates from sampling with the correct exclusions. MI+PB with $\pm0$ exclusions, which remove only the probe residue from contact counting, generated the source ensemble, biased on the $\chi_3$ dihedrals of the three disulfides. Production used $\pm2$ exclusions, which also remove the two residues on either side.

Four structures selected across the source ensemble, one per replica, seeded three matched conditions from identical coordinates. These were $\pm2$ MI+PB, $\pm2$ MI and unbiased MD, the last testing what the generated diversity retains under the force field alone. Each stage ran four 250 ns replicas, so a route consisting of the diversity stage and one $\pm2$ condition totals 2\,$\mu$s of production, 500-fold less simulated time than the Anton reference. All ensembles were projected without refitting onto the Anton C$\alpha$ PCA, and occupancy of the second basin was followed pooled, per replica and in 10 ns windows. These densities come from uniform frame counts, so they measure coverage and retention, not populations. Start selection and projection details are given in \cref{app:biofeat-bpti}.

\paragraph{MoPrP.}
MoPrP compares pH 4 with pH 7 under one experimental restraint. Both systems were built from the same AlphaFold3 model of mouse PrP 122--230\supercite{abramsonAccurateStructurePrediction2024} with protonation fixed at each nominal pH by PDB2PQR and PROPKA.\supercite{dolinskyPDB2PQRExpandingUpgrading2007,olssonPROPKA3ConsistentTreatment2011} Both were restrained by the same 77 residue-wise $\ln P_f$ values, recovered by exPfact from the HDX-MS data of Moulick et al.\supercite{skinnerEstimatingConstraintsProtection2019,moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} The paired simulations therefore compare how the force field at each protonation responds to one restraint.

MI+PB at each pH generated candidates, with the bias on five collective variables placed on the features of the partially unfolded forms (PUFs). These are the $\beta1$--$\beta2$ sheet contacts, the displacements of $\beta1$ and of the $\alpha1$--$\beta2$ unit, and the helicity of $\alpha1$ and of the C-terminal end of $\alpha3$. To follow how these states persist and connect, unbiased trajectories were seeded from three basins of each MI+PB ensemble on a Cartesian C$\alpha$ PCA fitted to the 20 NMR models of PDB 2L39. Twelve seeds per basin were each run at both target pH values, so every seed starts from the same frame at pH 4 and pH 7, for 12.48\,$\mu$s of production in total. Preparation, restrained sampling and the paired seed protocol are given in \cref{app:biofeat-moprp}.

\paragraph{TeaA.}
TeaA asks whether metadiffusion recovers known populations with no MD structure in any run. The target is synthetic HDX uptake from MD frames at 40\,\% open and 60\,\% closed, made with the same forward model that guides the generation, so the comparison carries no forward-model error. Generation saw the sequence, Boltz-2's own MSA-conditioned prediction as its only template, and the uptake. The MD open and closed structures entered only the scoring.

An unguided control from the same seed structures was reweighted in the same way, so the gain from guidance is measured against the generator's own coverage. States come from $\theta$ (open below $-5^\circ$, closed above $+5^\circ$, otherwise intermediate), and recovery is $100(1-\sqrt{\mathrm{JSD}})$ over the three states as in \cref{ch:jaxent}. Both are evaluation measures and never enter a loss. How sharply uptake agreement distinguishes open fractions sets how closely the recovered populations can be read. To measure it, mixtures of open and closed structures close to the MD references were scored against the target with the same forward model, at open fractions from 0 to 1. Target construction and control settings are given in \cref{app:biofeat-teaa-evaluation}, and reference checks in \cref{app:biofeat-teaa-checks}.

\subsection{MoPrP State Assignment and Dynamics}
\label{sec:biofeat-moprp-analysis}

The PUF descriptions combine local change in $\beta1$ with displacement of whole segments, so the MoPrP trajectories were followed on two planes. The PCA plane places frames by global Cartesian displacement from the NMR models, the projection on which the seeds were selected. The dihedral plane used for MoPrP in \cref{ch:jaxent} plots the Ramachandran dihedral RMSD to 2L39 (dRMSD) against the dihedral deviation of $\beta1$, which separates local change in $\beta1$ from global disorder.

PUF1 and PUF2 are experimental forms, defined by which segments lose protection.\supercite{moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015} \Cref{tab:biofeat-puf-definitions} distinguishes these descriptions from the rules used to identify candidates in simulated frames. To test the descriptions, we measured how far each segment moved relative to the core. Each frame was superposed on the disulfide-proximal core, and a segment counted as displaced when its core-fit RMSD exceeded the 99th percentile of Folded frames. The RMSD after superposing the segment on itself distinguishes rigid displacement from internal disorder. A segment that moves as a rigid body keeps a low self-fit RMSD. Threshold calibration and segment definitions are given in \cref{app:biofeat-moprp-assignment,tab:biofeat-moprp-segments}.

Segment rules test each PUF signature independently and can overlap (\cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-rules}). For analyses requiring one assignment per frame, structural clusters resolve overlaps in a fixed priority order (\cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-clusters}). The PUF2 cluster additionally requires $\beta1$ displacement and takes precedence over PUF1; Folded-like is the residual class. These assignments are distinct from the landscape regions and historical seed labels, whose definitions are given in \cref{tab:biofeat-moprp-regions,tab:biofeat-moprp-basins}.

Seeded runs start away from equilibrium, so their frame counts reflect where they began. Markov state models (MSMs) estimate stationary populations from the transitions the runs make.\supercite{prinzMarkovModelsMolecular2011} The MSM used 100 microstates from $k$-means on 10 Cartesian PCs fitted to unbiased MD, with a reversible maximum-likelihood fit at a lag of 5 ns. Not every macrostate passed the Chapman--Kolmogorov test, so we used the MSM to estimate stationary populations and did not interpret its transition rates (\cref{app:biofeat-moprp-msm}). Opening free energies follow the EX2 relation from the stationary probabilities of open and closed states (\cref{eq:bg-opening-free-energy}), which places them on the scale of the experimental values.

To describe the direction of motion separately from stationary populations, drift was measured directly as the mean displacement per nanosecond on each projection (\cref{app:biofeat-moprp-drift}). Drift describes motion in the projection, not in the full coordinates. Because every seed ran at both pH values, the pH 7 minus pH 4 change in drift compares the same 72 seeds.
