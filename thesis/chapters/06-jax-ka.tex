\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:jaxka-motivation}

Protonation links a protein's chemical environment to its response to pH. Burial, hydrogen bonding and nearby charges alter the relative stability of a titratable group's protonated and deprotonated forms, and shift its $\mathrm{p}K_a$ from the value measured in an unstructured peptide.\supercite{grimsleySummaryMeasuredPK2009} Binding can change these interactions, so the same group may titrate differently in the free protein and the complex. Differences between these states connect protonation to the pH dependence of binding affinity.\supercite{onufrievProtonationPKChanges2013}

Designing this pH dependence requires reconciling the information used for sequence generation with that needed to predict protonation. Protonation depends on side-chain environments, while inverse-folding models such as ProteinMPNN propose sequences from backbone coordinates without specifying side-chain conformations.\supercite{dauparasRobustDeepLearningbased2022} This backbone representation is shared by variants including SolubleMPNN and PottsMPNN.\supercite{dauparasRobustDeepLearningbased2022,birnbaumBeyondNativeSequence2026} A $\mathrm{p}K_a$ predictor that accepts backbone coordinates and sequence could help rank their proposed mutations by predicted protonation behaviour without reconstructing side chains for every candidate.

\subsection{Computational Titration Models}
\label{sec:jaxka-titration-models}

PROPKA illustrates the chemical information that such a backbone predictor would need to infer. It estimates a group's $\mathrm{p}K_a$ by adding empirical contributions from desolvation, hydrogen bonding and charge--charge interactions to a model-compound value. These contributions make individual predictions interpretable and inexpensive to calculate from an atomic structure.\supercite{liVeryFastEmpirical2005} The predictions depend on the supplied conformation and can fail when protonation accompanies structural rearrangement.\supercite{olssonPROPKA3ConsistentTreatment2011}

Within a fixed conformation, protonation at one site also changes the electrostatic environment experienced by the others. PypKa accounts for these interactions by combining Poisson--Boltzmann calculations of electrostatic energies with Monte Carlo sampling of protonation states. Sampling across pH yields titration curves from which $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values are obtained. The calculation holds the protein conformation fixed, while its dielectric model approximates the response of the protein and solvent. It captures coupling between protonation states within the supplied structure, but does not explicitly sample structural rearrangements.\supercite{reisPypKaFlexiblePython2020}

When those rearrangements alter protonation preferences, conformation and protonation must be sampled together. Constant-pH molecular dynamics allows protonation states to change as the protein moves, whereas conventional molecular dynamics holds the assigned states fixed. Changes in protonation can then alter the interactions governing motion, and motion can change the relative stability of protonation states. Accuracy depends on the physical model and sufficient sampling of both processes.\supercite{deoliveiraConstantPHMolecular2022} For mutation screening, our aim is to score candidate sequences without running these coupled simulations for every proposal.

pKAI accelerates fixed-structure prediction by learning the relationship between atomic environments and calculated $\mathrm{p}K_a$ shifts. Its neural network uses the residue type and a distance-weighted representation of nearby nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur atoms. Training on PypKa calculations allows it to approximate those predictions without repeating the electrostatic calculation and protonation-state sampling. The related pKAI+ model uses the same theoretical labels but penalises large predicted shifts. This penalty biases predictions towards the model-compound reference. Agreement with the computational labels and agreement with experimental $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values remain separate tests.\supercite{reisFastInterpretableDeep2022}

Experimental measurements can also supervise learned predictors directly. KaML uses CatBoost decision trees and graph attention networks trained against PKAD-3. For the graph model, pretraining uses $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values calculated by constant-pH molecular dynamics, while additional AlphaFold structures expand the structural examples associated with residues whose experimental shifts exceed two $\mathrm{p}K_a$ units in magnitude. On the authors' held-out tests, both models improve on PROPKA3, and the tree model is more accurate. Comparisons with published pKAI and PypKa errors use different test sets.\supercite{shenKaMLsPredictingProtein2025} A direct comparison requires a common experimental test set after removing entries used to train any of the models.

Even on a common test set, overall error can conceal whether a predictor captures the effect of the protein environment. Experimental datasets contain many solvent-exposed groups whose $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values lie close to their model-compound references. A null model that assigns these reference values without considering structure can achieve errors comparable to more elaborate predictors.\supercite{stantonBenchmarkingPKaPrediction2008,reisFastInterpretableDeep2022} We compare predictors against this baseline and assess large shifts separately to distinguish overall accuracy from the ability to recover departures from model-compound behaviour.

For binding, the relevant departure is the change between the complex and its isolated partners. We investigate whether backbone geometry and sequence retain enough information to predict both individual-state $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values and these binding-induced shifts. Training pairs each complex with its isolated partners held at their bound conformations, so the comparison isolates the predicted effect of the partner without including structural relaxation. Paired supervision makes this change an explicit prediction target.
