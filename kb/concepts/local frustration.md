## Definition

Local frustration is the presence, in a folded protein, of interactions that conflict with the stabilising ones. A folded protein does not resolve all conflicting interactions, so its structure remains locally frustrated ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c1]]). About 40% of native contacts are minimally frustrated and form the core ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c2]]), and about 10% are highly frustrated and cluster in surface patches ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c3]]). Highly frustrated patches are enriched in specific binding regions ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c4]]), in catalytic sites ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c5]]) and, in allosteric proteins, in the regions that change structure between states ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c7]]).

## Why it matters here

Frustration links the landscape to dynamics. The review links local frustration to protein dynamics ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c8]]), and local frustration drives interconversion between native-state conformations at the bottom of the folding landscape ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c13]]). The frustrated sites are where function is encoded locally, at the cost of stability ([[Function conflicts with folding, so sequences encode function locally at sites that sacrifice stability.]]), and the evolutionary signal at the same sites is the [[dark energy]]. The funnel-shaped landscape on which frustration acts is in [[conformational free-energy landscape]], and variants that change frustration act as a [[population shift]] ([[dynamic disease mechanism]]).

Frustration depends on context. It is larger for a protein studied on its own than in its complex, because the partner's compensating interactions are missing ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c28]], [[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c29]]; [[Frustration depends on a protein's context, so frustrated interactions change with partners and environment.]]). That is why a frustrated protein studied in isolation can show dynamics that its biological environment would change. Functional constraints can conflict with fold stability, while a binding partner can satisfy contacts left frustrated in isolation. These are complementary parts of the folding–function argument, so environmental dependence does not imply that every partner increases frustration.

## Assumptions and limits

- The Frustratometer needs an input structure and does not capture every biologically relevant energetic conflict, so frustration computed from one structure infers dynamics and does not measure them. The authors propose extending it to generated ensembles ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c27]]).
- Parra et al. is a review. Cite its primary studies for specific mechanisms.
