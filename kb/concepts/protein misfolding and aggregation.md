## Definition

Protein misfolding is a protein's departure from its native state into non-native conformations that can self-associate into oligomers and amyloid fibrils. In neurodegenerative diseases, proteins with no obvious similarity all misfold into intermolecular β-sheet-rich structures. The process, its intermediates, its end products and its main features are remarkably similar across these diseases. The oligomers are highly dynamic and exist in equilibrium with monomers and fibrils. Amyloid aggregates of misfolded proteins are a common pathological hallmark of neurodegenerative disease. Yet nucleation, the crucial first step, is poorly understood, because instruments cannot resolve such transient events in space and time.

## Why it matters here

Misfolding diseases are the strongest case in Section 1.1 that static structural biology is not enough. Fibril end states can be solved at atomic resolution, but the [[high-energy intermediate|intermediates]] that start aggregation are transient, heterogeneous and never crystallise. The case studies are [[alpha-synuclein]] and the [[prion protein]]. Misfolding is the kinetic, often irreversible end of a [[dynamic disease mechanism]]: a mutation or environment that shifts weight toward an aggregation-prone state can have an outsized effect because aggregation is nonlinear in concentration.
