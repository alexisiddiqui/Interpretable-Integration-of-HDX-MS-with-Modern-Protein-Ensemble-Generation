## Definition

In single-particle cryo-EM, individual macromolecular complexes are frozen in a thin layer of vitreous ice and imaged. A 3D structure is then reconstructed from projection images of many particles in different orientations. Advances in electron detection and image processing have brought its resolution close to that of [[X-ray crystallography]], without the need for a crystal.

## Why it matters here

Cryo-EM is the second static method in Section 1.1. Its sharpest example is [[alpha-synuclein]]: cryo-EM has solved the Lewy body fibril structure at atomic resolution, yet the soluble protein is a disordered [[structural ensemble]]. A high-resolution end state therefore does not reveal the states that lead to it.

Claims that use this term: [[Structural biology has made tremendous strides in solving static protein structures by X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM and NMR.]], [[Alpha-synuclein is an unfolded monomer, yet cryo-EM has solved its Lewy body fibril structures at atomic resolution.]], [[Cryo-EM fibrils are helical but the solution state is disordered, so aggregation-prone states can escape standard methods.]], [[Orthogonal to X-ray, cryo-EM and NMR, HDX-MS probes protein dynamics in solution under diverse conditions.]].
