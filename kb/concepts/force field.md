## Definition

A force field is the model of the potential energy $U(x)$ of a conformation, and of the forces that are its negative gradient. [[molecular dynamics|MD]] uses the forces at every time step to update positions and velocities ([[@hollingsworthMolecularDynamicsSimulation2018#^c5]]). Empirical force fields are fixed functional forms fitted to data; they are efficient but inherently approximate ([[@hollingsworthMolecularDynamicsSimulation2018#^c2]], [[@unkeBiomolecularDynamicsMachinelearned2024#^c2]]). A machine-learned force field (MLFF) is a model trained on ab initio reference data to predict energies and forces ([[@unkeBiomolecularDynamicsMachinelearned2024#^c1]]). It replaces the empirical $U(x)$ inside MD; it does not change how the simulation samples.

## Assumptions and limits

- A converged equilibrium simulation samples the [[Boltzmann distribution]] of its force field, not of the real protein (ours). Force-field error therefore becomes error in the populations of the simulated ensemble, and in the prior that reweighting starts from ([[@bmsb2012ch12#^c13]]).
- An MLFF improves accuracy, not coverage. The sampling problem ([[equilibration and convergence]], [[sampling coverage]]) is unchanged.
- An MLFF is not a [[Boltzmann generator]]. The first learns energies and forces for simulation; the second generates configurations and must reweight them to the Boltzmann distribution ([[@noeBoltzmannGeneratorsSampling2019#^c3]]). The confirmation cites the two together; keep them apart.
- **Potential energy as a score.** $U(x)$ scores one conformation under the model, for example to flag clashes or strained geometry after minimisation. A low $U(x)$ does not make a state populated, because population depends on the free energy of the whole basin, entropy included ([[Boltzmann distribution#^eq-free-energy-entropy]]). Energies are comparable only within one system, force field and protonation state (ours). Unlike a [[statistical potential]], $U(x)$ is a physical model, but it inherits every approximation of the force field.
- Functional forms, parameterisation and water models are Chapter 2 material, if needed at all.

## Why it matters here

Every MD-based candidate ensemble in ValDX and jaxENT inherits its force field's bias. That is one reason simulated populations need checking against HDX-MS, and why the starting weights in reweighting are a prior rather than the answer (introduction §1.3).

Claims that use this term: [[MD is computationally expensive and may only approximate true dynamics due to force-field inaccuracies and limited sampling.]], [[Sufficiently sampled equilibrium MD targets the Boltzmann distribution of its approximate force field, not the protein's true distribution.]], [[Machine-learned force fields learn energies and forces for simulation, whereas Boltzmann generators generate configurations and reweight them to the Boltzmann distribution.]], [[Ensembles can be generated by learning the physical landscape of protein systems directly from existing simulations.]].
