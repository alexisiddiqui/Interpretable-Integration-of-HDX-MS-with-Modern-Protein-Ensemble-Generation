## Definition

A high-energy intermediate is a conformational state that lies above the ground state on the [[conformational free-energy landscape]]. It is therefore sparsely and often transiently populated at equilibrium. These elusive states often play critical roles in catalysis, folding and aggregation. In amyloid formation, a partially folded, pre-molten-globule-like intermediate quickly initiates nucleation, and the conformational conversion of oligomers can be the rate-limiting step. Their transience and low population make them hard to isolate and study at high resolution.

## Why it matters here

These states are the clearest case where a static structure fails. The ground state and the end state can both be solved, yet the states that control the kinetics in between cannot. Recovering them means capturing a minor population in a [[structural ensemble]], which is the regime where generation has to find the state and reweighting has to give it the right weight. The partially unfolded forms of the [[prion protein]] (PUF1 and PUF2 in the MoPrP figure) are the thesis's example.
