# Voice: spelling, punctuation and grammar

Surface rules for prose in my voice. The argumentative habits are in [[style]]; model passages are in [[examples]]. Punctuation and grammar here mainly make logical relationships explicit.

The single most characteristic habit: **place a claim alongside the condition governing its validity.**

## Spelling

- British spelling throughout: *behaviour, optimisation, characterisation, normalisation, hypothesise, modelling, analyse, centre*.
- *-ise* not *-ize*; *-yse* not *-yze*.
- Keep project and method names as published: ValDX, jaxENT, Work Done, Apparent Work.

## Punctuation

- **Commas organise layered reasoning.** Open with a purpose or condition ("To distinguish…", "Given…", "While…", "Within a single ensemble,"), then give the main claim, then attach qualifications. Avoid comma splices: drafts occasionally produce them, and the finished manuscripts do not. Join independent clauses with a conjunction, a semicolon, or a full stop.
- **Parentheses carry supporting precision**: abbreviations, numerical results, figure references, brief definitions. Keep the main clause for interpretation. Do not put the claim itself in parentheses.
- **Colons introduce structured explanation**: named alternatives, component lists, specifications. Paired colons in consecutive sentences can set up matching structures (evidence, then the claims it tests).
- **Semicolons** connect closely related clauses, sparingly.
- **Hyphens bind technical compounds** used attributively: *ensemble-average, residue-level, forward-model, peptide-level, individual-frame, ground-truth availability*. Hyphenate consistently within a document.
- **Questions** only to expose an unresolved scientific distinction or frame a research objective. No rhetorical questions for effect.

## Grammar

- **Subordinate clauses carry scientific conditions.** *because* (cause), *although* (concession), *while* (contrast), *when* (scope), *provided* (dependency). These are the main way caution enters a sentence.
- **Lead with the governing condition** when a result changes meaning across contexts, and use parallel construction so the change is visible:
  "Within a single ensemble, greater X reflects… When comparing candidate ensembles at comparable predictive agreement, smaller X instead indicates…"
- **Voice follows function.** Active "we" for investigative choices; results and metrics as subjects for reported behaviour; passive when the procedure or object matters more than the actor.
- **Tense follows function.** Past tense for analyses performed and results observed; present tense for definitions, interpretations and what a quantity measures.
- **Parallel lists stay grammatically parallel**, e.g. repeated "that…": "that the objective recovers…, that it beats…, and that its uncertainty estimates remain…".
- **Sentence length creates emphasis.** Long explanatory sentences followed by a short declarative that sets a limit or consequence. Shorten towards the conclusion of a sequence, and earn any "therefore".

## Avoid

- Empty transitions: *Importantly, Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Notably, Interestingly*.
- Intensifiers and unconditional claims: *simply, clearly, ensures, successfully, fundamental, robust, novel, powerful, crucial*.
- Synonym-swapping a technical noun for variety.
- Comma splices and run-on qualifier chains; split into a long sentence plus a short one instead.
