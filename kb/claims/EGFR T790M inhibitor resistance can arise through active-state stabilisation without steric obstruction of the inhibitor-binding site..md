## Argument

EGFR T790M inhibitor resistance can arise through stabilisation of the active conformation without steric obstruction of the inhibitor-binding site. This is Sutto and Gervasio's interpretation of the T790M gatekeeper mutation ([[@suttoEffectsOncogenicMutations2013#^c3]]). The example shows how populations of familiar structures can explain behaviour left unresolved by comparison of the structures alone ([[population shift]]).

## Caveats

The claim concerns the T790M interpretation in Sutto and Gervasio. The PKA example is retained in [[A single mutation can leave the crystal structure unchanged yet strongly alter function.]], while IGPS provides a distinct kinetic example in [[allostery]]. Neither establishes that all mutations preserve the fold or avoid steric effects.

## Wording history

Primary wording follows `thesis/chapters/01-introduction.tex#L76`. The historical wording comes from this note before revision, with its original source in `origin` and its evidence links retained in `cites`.

The primary wording assigns the steric-hindrance distinction to the EGFR case that supports it. The previous wording combined EGFR, PKA and IGPS, and its requirement for sampling many distributions was the thesis's inference.

### Historical title

> Mutations create neither steric clashes nor new folds but shift conformational ensembles, which needs sampling of many distributions.

### Historical argument

> Sutto and Gervasio report that the T790M gatekeeper mutation in EGFR does not appear to act by steric hindrance with ATP-competitive inhibitors, but by stabilising the active conformation. Lisi and Loria describe a PKA mutation that leaves the crystal structure and NMR shifts nearly unchanged but disrupts catalysis, and IGPS activation driven by millisecond [[protein dynamics|dynamics]] without large structural rearrangements.

### Historical caveats

> No cited passage states that these cases need sampling of many distributions. That clause is the thesis's own inference from the cases. The Lisi passages cover two enzymes and the Sutto passage one EGFR mutation, so the sentence generalises from three examples.
