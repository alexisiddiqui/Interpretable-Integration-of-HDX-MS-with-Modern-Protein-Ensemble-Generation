## Argument

Marciano describes HDX-MS as complementing NMR and crystallography, and describes differential HDX-MS analysis of a protein under different conditions, such as apo versus holo, as an important tool. James says HDX-MS studies typically take a protein under native conditions in aqueous buffer, and that internal exchange reporters enable more robust comparisons where solution conditions are not matched, for example with chaotropes or buffer additives.

## Caveats

The earlier wording ('without size limits', 'uniquely') was dropped. James p16 says the size limit is set by the platform's ability to resolve and detect all the peptides, and no checked paper says HDX-MS is uniquely able to probe dynamics. James ^c2 and Marciano ^c2 support the separate statement about large and heterogeneous systems.

Solution conditions set the intrinsic exchange rate and can change the exchange regime, so comparisons across unmatched conditions need matched conditions or an internal exchange reporter (James p3, p35).
