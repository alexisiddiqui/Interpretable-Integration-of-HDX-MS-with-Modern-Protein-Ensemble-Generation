## Argument

Under EX2 an amide opens and closes many times before it exchanges, and each opening is only an opportunity for exchange (James; Vinciauskaite). The time at which a site becomes deuterated is therefore set by how often it is exchange-competent and by its intrinsic rate, not by how long one opening lasts. ValDX states the consequence: slow exchange can reflect a low population of exchange-competent conformations rather than slow structural motion (step ours from the cited kinetics).

## Caveats

Holds under EX2. Under EX1 the observed rate is the opening rate, so labelling time does then report on a motion's kinetics.
