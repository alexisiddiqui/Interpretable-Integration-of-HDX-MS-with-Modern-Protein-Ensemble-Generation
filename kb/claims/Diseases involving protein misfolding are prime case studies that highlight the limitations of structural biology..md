## Argument

Soto and Pritzkow describe misfolded oligomers as an ill-defined, heterogeneous group of species, and say that their heterogeneity and rapid interconversion have made high-resolution structural information very difficult to obtain. They also say the absence of high-resolution structural information for PrPSc has limited understanding of prion strains. Mukherjee et al. say amyloid nucleation is poorly understood because of the spatial and temporal resolution limit of instruments for detecting transient events. These are cases where methods, including structural ones, have been limited.

## Caveats

Soto and Pritzkow make their points about misfolded oligomers and PrPSc specifically, and Mukherjee's point is about instruments in general rather than structural biology; the sentence generalizes to protein-misfolding diseases as 'prime case-studies', which no passage says. The Biancaniello paper (@biancanielloStructureDynamicsMisfolding2025 p1 ^c2, p8 ^c9) makes a similar point about X-ray crystallography, NMR and cryo-EM, but it is not cited for this sentence.

The cited passages cover misfolded oligomers (Soto and Pritzkow p2 ^c3, ^c5), PrPSc (p5 ^c6) and amyloid nucleation (Mukherjee p4 ^c4) only, so the general statement about misfolding diseases goes beyond them.
