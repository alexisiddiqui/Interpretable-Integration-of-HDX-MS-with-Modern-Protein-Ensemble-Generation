## Argument

Model-compound pKa values put His at 6.54 and Asp and Glu at 3.67 and 4.25 ([[@thurlkillPKValuesIonizable2006#^c2]]; Asp later revised to 3.94). By the Henderson–Hasselbalch form fitted to those titrations ([[@thurlkillPKValuesIonizable2006#^c3]]; [[pKa#^eq-hh]]), a site is about 99% protonated two units below its pKa and about 1% protonated two units above it. Lowering the pH from 7 to 4 therefore protonates almost every unperturbed His and a third to two thirds of Asp and Glu, and each protonation adds one positive charge to $Q$, because acids are uncharged and bases charged below their pK ([[@paceProteinIonizableGroups2009#^c1]]) ([[pKa#^eq-net-charge]]).

## Caveats

The statement is about unperturbed groups. A [[pKa shift]] can move a buried His or Asp outside this window, which is the point of MoPrP H186 ([[prion protein]]). The fractions are step ours from the model values.
