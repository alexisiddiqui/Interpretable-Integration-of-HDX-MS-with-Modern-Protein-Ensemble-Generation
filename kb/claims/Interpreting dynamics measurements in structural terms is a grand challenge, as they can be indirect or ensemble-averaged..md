## Argument

Bradshaw et al. state that the mechanistic implications of [[HDX-MS]] data are hard to interpret because of limited spatial resolution and a lack of quantitative tools, that the major challenge is translating HDX data into structural information, that the experiment measures deuteration averaged over peptide fragments, and that the data reflect an [[structural ensemble]] of conformations. McAllister and Konermann find that interpreting HDX data in a protein conformational context is surprisingly difficult.

## Caveats

Bradshaw et al. and McAllister and Konermann frame this as the challenge for HDX data specifically, not for structural biology in general. The title is the author's sentence: 'grand challenge' corresponds to 'major challenge' (Bradshaw) and 'surprisingly difficult' (McAllister), and 'indirect' to peptide-level averaging and the need for a model to interpret the data.

The word indirect is not stated in the cited passages (it is the author's gloss on peptide-level averaging), and McAllister does not use the words ensemble-averaged or indirect in the checked passage.
