## Argument

Soto and Pritzkow describe [[protein misfolding and aggregation|misfolded oligomers]] as an ill-defined, heterogeneous group of species, and say that their heterogeneity and rapid interconversion have made high-resolution structural information very difficult to obtain. They also say the absence of high-resolution structural information for PrPSc has limited understanding of prion strains. Mukherjee et al. say amyloid nucleation is poorly understood because of the spatial and temporal resolution limit of instruments for detecting transient events.

## Caveats

Soto and Pritzkow discuss misfolded oligomers and PrPSc. Mukherjee's resolution limit concerns detection of transient nucleation events, rather than every structural measurement of a protein-misfolding disease. Biancaniello et al. also discuss the difficulty of capturing dynamic intermediate species with X-ray crystallography, NMR and cryo-EM ([[@biancanielloStructureDynamicsMisfolding2025#^c2]], [[@biancanielloStructureDynamicsMisfolding2025#^c9]]).
