## Argument

(own) The thesis reads the prion landscape as a memory. Folding and unfolding follow different pathways at pH 4 and the kinetics depend on initial conditions ([[At pH 4 the mouse prion protein folds along a pathway distinct from its unfolding pathway, so its kinetics depend on initial conditions.]]), so the state reached carries the history of the condition applied. Conformational states of prions are independently proposed to record environmental context ([[@yangMolecularMechanismEnvironmental2026#^c2]]) and to store environmental experience ([[@johnsonPrionlikeProteinsFacilitators2026#^c2]]).

The comparison with a memristor is the thesis's own analogy. In an oxide defect the charge transition barrier is set by the crossing point of two potential-energy curves ([[@waldhoerMinimumEnergyPaths2019#^c1]]), and an applied gate bias shifts the curves relative to each other ([[@waldhoerMinimumEnergyPaths2019#^c2]]). An applied condition such as urea concentration or pH plays the part of the gate bias, and interconversion between states needs it to reach the crossing point (panel B of `fig:intro-frustrated-memory`). See [[prion protein]].

## Caveats

No source states the memristor analogy or applies it to proteins. Waldhoer concerns oxide defects only, and Johnson and Meyer present their proposal for plants as speculation. Yang et al. is a preprint on a yeast non-amyloid prion.
