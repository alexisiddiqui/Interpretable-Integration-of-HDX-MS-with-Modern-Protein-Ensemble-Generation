## Argument

The HDX rate depends on the local environment of the amide hydrogen, including solvent accessibility and the hydrogen-bonding network ([[@vinciauskaiteFundamentalsHDXMS2023#^c2]]). These structural features modulate exchange in folded proteins ([[@narangHDXMSAnalyticalTool2020#^c3]]). Flexible and unfolded regions can exchange rapidly ([[@vinciauskaiteFundamentalsHDXMS2023#^c3]]), but exchange rate is not a direct measure of flexibility ([[HDX-MS]]).

## Wording history

Primary wording follows `thesis/chapters/01-introduction.tex#L110`. The historical wording comes from this note before revision, with its original source in `origin` and its evidence links retained in `cites`.

The primary wording names the local structural contributions. The earlier association between flexibility and rapid exchange remains as secondary wording, with the interpretation bounded by the protection-versus-flexibility distinction.

### Historical title

> The HDX rate depends on the local environment of the amide hydrogen, with more exposed and flexible regions exchanging faster.

### Historical argument

> Narang et al. state that in folded proteins the exchange rate is modulated by structural features. Vinciauskaite et al. list solvent accessibility and the hydrogen-bonding network among the factors setting the rate, and say unfolded, flexible and dynamic regions exchange on the millisecond timescale.
