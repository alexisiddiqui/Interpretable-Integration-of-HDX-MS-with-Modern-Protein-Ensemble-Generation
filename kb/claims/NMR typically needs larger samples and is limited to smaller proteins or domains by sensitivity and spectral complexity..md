## Argument

Kleckner and Foster state that the poor sensitivity of [[NMR]] makes it costly in time and quantity of material and places a relatively low upper limit on the size of molecules studied.

## Caveats

Spectral complexity is not given as a size limit: Kleckner and Foster note that spectral crowding is frequently cited as a limitation but present it as a consequence of NMR having many structural probes.

Kay (2011) is a likely mis-citation for this sentence: it is a perspective on NMR studies of protein structure and dynamics and says nothing about large sample quantities. It reports that methyl-TROSY NMR has been extended to complexes of 300 to 700 kDa (p2), which cuts against a size limit.
