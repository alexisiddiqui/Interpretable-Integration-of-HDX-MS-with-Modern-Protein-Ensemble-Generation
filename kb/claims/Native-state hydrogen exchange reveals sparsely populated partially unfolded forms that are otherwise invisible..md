## Argument

Englander describes native-state hydrogen exchange as revealing a previously unsuspected level of cooperative substructure ([[@englanderHydrogenExchangeModern1997#^c4]]). The partially unfolded intermediates it detects are normally invisible because their population is minimal ([[@englanderHydrogenExchangeModern1997#^c5]]). The fluctuations that set exchange rates are rare, unlike the fast fluctuations most dynamics studies address ([[@skinnerProteinHydrogenExchange2012#^c7]]). The point carries forward: [[HDX-MS]] is sensitive to exactly the sparse [[high-energy intermediate|high-energy states]] that static structures do not show.

## Caveats

Englander's results are for cytochrome c and similar small proteins under native-state HX by NMR, at residue resolution; HDX-MS reports at the peptide level (§1.2). The interpretation of which cooperative units open remains ambiguous ([[@englanderHydrogenExchangeModern1997#^c3]]).
