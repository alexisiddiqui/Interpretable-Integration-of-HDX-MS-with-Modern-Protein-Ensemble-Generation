## Argument

Hornemann and Glockshuber found an unfolding intermediate of mouse PrP(121–231) with a β-sheet CD spectrum that is populated only at acidic pH ([[@hornemannScrapielikeUnfoldingIntermediate1998#^c1]]); unfolding at pH 6.0 and 7.0 is apparent two-state ([[@hornemannScrapielikeUnfoldingIntermediate1998#^c3]]). Without denaturant the intermediate is in rapid equilibrium with the native state but small, about 0.2% at pH 4.0 and far less at pH 4.5 ([[@hornemannScrapielikeUnfoldingIntermediate1998#^c2]]). Its population peaks at 95% at pH 4.0 in 3.5 M urea and is not measurable above pH 5.5 ([[@hornemannScrapielikeUnfoldingIntermediate1998#^c4]]). Acidic pH makes the intermediate accessible, and destabilisation populates it ([[prion protein]]).

## Caveats

The construct is a truncated domain (121–231), not the full-length MoPrP of the HDX-MS data. The intermediate forms with an apparent pKa of about 4.5 ([[@hornemannScrapielikeUnfoldingIntermediate1998]] ^c5), consistent with acidic residues titrating, but the paper does not name the residues. Singh and Udgaonkar later did ([[Protonation of H186 or D201 drives MoPrP misfolding, and HDX-MS shows helix 1 and the α1–β2 loop become more dynamic.]]).
