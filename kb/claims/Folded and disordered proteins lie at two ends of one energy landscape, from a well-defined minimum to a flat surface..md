## Argument

The topography of the [[conformational free-energy landscape]] sets the probability of each conformation ([[@fisherConstructingEnsemblesIntrinsically2011#^c4]]). Folded proteins have a well-defined global minimum ([[@fisherConstructingEnsemblesIntrinsically2011#^c5]]), and [[intrinsically disordered protein|intrinsically disordered proteins]] have relatively flat landscapes ([[@fisherConstructingEnsemblesIntrinsically2011#^c1]]). Galpern et al. treat a folded protein as having about one effective conformation per residue ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c5]]) and present IDPs as retaining substantial native conformational entropy ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c10]]). Read together, the two are ends of one axis rather than different kinds of object.

## Caveats

Neither source states the "one axis" framing; it is our reading of the two. Many proteins are partly folded, so the axis is a simplification for motivation, not a classification.
