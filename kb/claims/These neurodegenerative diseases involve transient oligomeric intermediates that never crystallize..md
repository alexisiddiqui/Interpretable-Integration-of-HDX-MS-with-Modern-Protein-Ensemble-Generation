## Argument

Mukhtasimova et al. study intermediate states priming opening of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor channel, not neurodegenerative oligomers. Mukherjee et al. say early aggregation is hard to study because oligomers are heterogeneous and transient, which supports the first half of the sentence.

## Caveats

No checked passage says these intermediates 'never crystallize'. Mukherjee supports only that the oligomers of neurodegeneration-associated proteins are heterogeneous and transient (p3).

Closest passage for the second half: @sotoProteinMisfoldingAggregation2018 p2 (^c3, ^c4, ^c5), which describes misfolded oligomers as heterogeneous, highly dynamic and in equilibrium with monomers and fibrils, and says high-resolution structural information for them is very difficult to obtain. Soto is not among this sentence's cites, and none of these passages mentions crystallization, so 'never crystallize' remains unsupported. The author decides whether to cite it.
