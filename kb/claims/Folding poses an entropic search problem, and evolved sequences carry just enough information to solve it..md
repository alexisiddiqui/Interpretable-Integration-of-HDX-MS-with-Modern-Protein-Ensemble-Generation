## Argument

Levinthal's paradox is the entropic challenge of finding the native conformation among the alternatives; Galpern et al. quantify it as the information gained per residue on folding ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c13]]). Molecular information theory postulates that this matches the information evolution gains in selecting foldable sequences ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c3]]). Empirical estimates agree: the effective number of unfolded conformations per residue sits close to the square root of the alphabet size ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c6]]), so sequences hold just enough information for folding to be a tractable search.

## Caveats

The equality is a postulate, consistent with the data but not derived from them. Sites are treated as independent. The argument concerns folding only; function needs information beyond it ([[@galpernInformationtheoreticArgumentRestriction2026#^c9]]).
