## Argument

Singh and Udgaonkar find that of the three His residues only H186 strongly destabilises MoPrP and speeds misfolding when protonated ([[@singhUnravelingMolecularMechanism2016#^c1]]); H186 is protonated at pH 4 but not at pH 7 ([[@singhUnravelingMolecularMechanism2016#^c4]]). D201 is the second site. It forms a salt bridge with R155, and neutralising either partner destabilises the protein and speeds misfolding ([[@singhUnravelingMolecularMechanism2016#^c2]]). HDX-MS locates the change: helix 1 and the loop between helix 1 and β-strand 2 become more dynamic ([[@singhUnravelingMolecularMechanism2016#^c3]]). This makes MoPrP a case where specific titratable sites, not the overall charge, decide the conformational outcome ([[prion protein]], [[pKa shift]]).

## Caveats

Mouse numbering (H186 = human H187). Check the exact regions and conditions in the blocks before quoting them in the thesis.
