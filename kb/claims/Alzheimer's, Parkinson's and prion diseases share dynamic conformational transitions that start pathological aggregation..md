## Argument

Soto and Pritzkow say the proteins of Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and prion diseases all misfold from their native states into beta-sheet-rich structures, by a process whose intermediates, end-products and main features are remarkably similar. Mukherjee et al. say aggregates of misfolded proteins are a common pathological hallmark of all neurodegenerative diseases, and that a partially folded pre-molten-globule-like conformation is a key intermediate that quickly initiates nucleation.

## Caveats

'Dynamic conformational transitions' is the author's gloss on this misfolding; Soto describes the shared misfolding process and its seeding-nucleation mechanism rather than using that wording. Mukherjee (a review of liquid-liquid phase separation in amyloid nucleation) states the shared misfolded-aggregate hallmark in general (p2); its key-intermediate passage (p4) concerns alpha-synuclein, tau, amyloid-beta and TDP-43 and does not name prion disease, so a shared conformational mechanism across all three diseases is only partly stated.
