## Argument

Galpern et al. define dark energy as the difference between the physical folding free energies and the evolutionary free energies inferred from sequences ([[@galpernProbingDarkEnergy2026#^c1]]). A variant that affects only folding shifts the folding and evolutionary energies together and leaves the dark energy unchanged ([[@galpernProbingDarkEnergy2026#^c14]]), whereas a variant that affects a function beyond folding is expected to change it significantly ([[@galpernProbingDarkEnergy2026#^c16]]; panels A to C of `fig:intro-evolutionary-funnel`). A two-component mixture separates single-site variants into mainly folding-related (74.7%) and mainly function-related (25.3%) groups ([[@galpernProbingDarkEnergy2026#^c5]]). The evolutionary score on its own therefore flags a variant without showing which of the two is affected. See [[dark energy]].

## Caveats

The sentence that the score combines folding and function is this note's reading of the definition and of Fig. 1 in the paper. The 74.7% and 25.3% split depends on a Gaussian-mixture threshold and on the folding-energy method.
