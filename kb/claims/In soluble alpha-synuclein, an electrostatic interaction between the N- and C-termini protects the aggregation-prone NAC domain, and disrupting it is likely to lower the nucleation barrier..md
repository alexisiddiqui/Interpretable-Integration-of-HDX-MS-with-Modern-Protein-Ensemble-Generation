## Argument

Mukherjee et al. say [[alpha-synuclein]] has an autoinhibitory conformation, held by an electrostatic interaction between its N- and C-termini, that protects the aggregation-prone NAC domain, and that disrupting it exposes NAC and is likely to lower the nucleation barrier.

Also stated in: milestones/2025-11-confirmation-of-status/introduction/biological_dynamics.tex#L36

Line 36 makes the second half of this claim: disruption of these fleeting contacts starts aggregation. It cites Mukherjee, whose p12 passages above support the disruption-starts-aggregation point but not that the contacts are 'fleeting' or on a picosecond timescale.

Also stated in: milestones/2025-11-confirmation-of-status/introduction/biological_dynamics.tex#L38

Line 38 restates the claim, saying the protective interactions occur on picosecond to millisecond timescales while pathology develops over years. Its two cited papers are Hollingsworth (no passage found about alpha-synuclein) and Karplus and McCammon, which says nothing about alpha-synuclein or NMR of such interactions.

## Caveats

The claim originally said that NMR showed protective picosecond interactions whose disruption starts aggregation. It now states only what Mukherjee et al. say.

Ahmed et al. refine alpha-synuclein ensembles against SAXS data and mention only that its aggregation is linked to Parkinson's disease. Sapienza and Lee review ps-ns NMR relaxation methods and do not discuss alpha-synuclein. Mukherjee describes the autoinhibitory N-/C-terminal interaction (citing other work) but does not say it was shown by NMR or occurs on picosecond timescales. In the neighbouring sentence on the same page, Mukherjee attributes NAC exposure to low pH or high salt in vitro, which weakens the reading of 'protective interactions whose disruption starts aggregation'. The NMR and picosecond parts of the claim are therefore unsupported by every checked paper.
