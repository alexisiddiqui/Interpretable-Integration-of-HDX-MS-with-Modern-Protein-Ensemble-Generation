## Argument

Parra et al. propose that local frustration drives interconversion between conformations at the bottom of the folding landscape ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c13]]). In thrombin, catalysis is linked to slow motions, not nanosecond ones ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c10]]), and an allosteric regulator acts through frustrated contacts ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c11]]). Independently, IGPS activation is driven by millisecond dynamics without large rearrangements ([[@lisiAllosteryEnzymeCatalysis2017#^c7]]), high-energy conformations play critical roles in catalysis, folding and aggregation ([[@biancanielloStructureDynamicsMisfolding2025#^c1]], [[@biancanielloStructureDynamicsMisfolding2025#^c11]]), and a single static structure is not enough to understand enzyme function ([[@casadevallAlphaFold2DeepLearning2023#^c3]]). Function therefore depends on how the native [[structural ensemble]] is populated.

## Caveats

The link from frustration to slow states rests on one well-studied case (thrombin) in a review; the other sources support slow, high-energy states without discussing frustration. The general coupling is our synthesis.
