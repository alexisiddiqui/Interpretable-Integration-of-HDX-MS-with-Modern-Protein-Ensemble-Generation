## Argument

Parra et al. describe the folded state as still a large ensemble of structures ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c32]]). Their example is hemoglobin: frozen in its crystal structure, it could not let oxygen in to bind ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c33]]). Lisi and Loria give the experimental counterpart: the distinct X-ray structures of PKA are now read as three time-averaged conformations of one [[structural ensemble]] ([[@lisiAllosteryEnzymeCatalysis2017#^c5]]). A crystal structure is therefore one member, or an average, of what the protein populates.

## Caveats

The hemoglobin sentence is a rhetorical illustration in a review, not a result; use it as framing. The primary evidence for oxygen entry through transient channels should be cited if the example goes into the thesis.
