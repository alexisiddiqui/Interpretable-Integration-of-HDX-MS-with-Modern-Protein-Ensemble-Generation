## Argument

At matched labelling times and intrinsic exchange conditions, greater [[deuterium uptake]] indicates less protection from exchange. The [[protection factor]] is the ratio of the intrinsic to the observed exchange rate, so a change in intrinsic chemistry must be distinguished from a change in protection. Exposure, hydrogen bonding and conformational populations can each affect protection; uptake does not directly measure flexibility (step ours from the cited protection-factor definition and interpretation limits).

## Caveats

The comparison requires matched intrinsic exchange chemistry and labelling times. The inference from uptake to protection does not identify which structural event changed protection ([[HDX-MS]]).

### Historical title

> Greater deuterium uptake indicates less protection from exchange, not directly greater flexibility.

### Historical argument

> Uptake is set by the protection factor ([[protection factor]]), the rate expected for a freely exposed hydrogen divided by the measured rate (Skinner et al.), which quantifies how far secondary and tertiary structure slow exchange (Smith et al.). Masson et al. (2017) state that the exact molecular reasons an amide exchanges at a given rate are still not clear, and Masson et al. (2019) warn that the simple experiment is not simple to interpret because the exchange mechanism is complex. Reading uptake as "flexibility" therefore adds an interpretation the measurement does not make (step ours).

### Historical caveats

> The origin sentence ("more exposed and flexible regions exchanging more rapidly") is supported as stated; this claim narrows how it should be read. The definition of the protection factor supports the first sentence, so the only inferred step is the contrast with flexibility.
