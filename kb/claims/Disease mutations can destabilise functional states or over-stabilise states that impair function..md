## Argument

The disease-associated mutations collected by Parra et al. can either increase or decrease local frustration. p53 R248Q raises frustration and conformational heterogeneity ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c20]]). AADC P330L makes the open catalytic loop less frustrated, favouring the open loop and impairing catalysis ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c18]]), desmin Glu353dup over-stabilises a dimer interface ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c19]]), and oncogene mutations can relieve frustration ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c24]]). Kinase cancer mutations may shift frustration between inactive and active states ([[@parraFrustrationPhysiologyMolecular2025#^c22]]), and in EGFR, L858R and T790M stabilise the active state ([[@suttoEffectsOncogenicMutations2013#^c2]], [[@suttoEffectsOncogenicMutations2013#^c3]]). These mutations can alter function by changing the relative stability of conformational states ([[dynamic disease mechanism]]).

## Caveats

Most cases are computational and correlational, gathered in a review; cite the primary studies (Bizzarri 2022, Bisello 2022, Castañeda 2023, Dixit and Verkhivker 2011). The two-directions framing is ours.
