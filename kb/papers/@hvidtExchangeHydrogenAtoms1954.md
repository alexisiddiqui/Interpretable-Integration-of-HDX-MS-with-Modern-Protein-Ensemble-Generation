# Exchange of Hydrogen Atoms in Insulin with Deuterium Atoms in Aqueous Solutions (1954)

## Main point

Pork insulin exchanged with 99.6% D2O at pH 3 and 37 °C replaces only 46 of its 92 exchangeable hydrogens even after 124 hours. The authors take the protected hydrogens to be backbone amides held in stable internal hydrogen bonds, and since the isolated oxidised A-chain exchanges all its peptide hydrogens, they tie that protection to the native tertiary structure.

## What I use from it

The origin of hydrogen exchange as a probe of protein structure, cited with the Linderstrøm-Lang group for the 1950s framework in which slow exchange reports on hydrogen bonding held by the folded state.

## Disagreements / limitations

The PDF text layer is letter-spaced OCR, so several quotes are copied in that letter-spaced form and the c5 quote only locates the A-chain passage. The structural reading (a double α-helix model) is a 1954 interpretation of a whole-protein count, with no site resolution.
