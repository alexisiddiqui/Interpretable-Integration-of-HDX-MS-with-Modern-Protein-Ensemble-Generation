# Hydrogen Exchange Identifies Native-State Motional Domains Important in Protein Folding (1993)

## Main point

In four destabilised BPTI mutants, amide exchange at pH 3.5 and 30 °C proceeds by two parallel pathways, small noncooperative native-state fluctuations and cooperative global unfolding. Exchange rates divide the protein into motional domains (slow exchange core, flexible loops, other secondary structure), and the slow exchange core coincides with the folding core.

## What I use from it

Support for reading slow exchange as cooperative unfolding (ΔΔG from rate ratios of the slowest amides, checked against calorimetry), together with the warning that faster native-state exchange comes from local fluctuations and does not track global stability.

## Disagreements / limitations

The paper never uses the term "protection factor" or "EX2". It ties the slowest amides to global unfolding free energy, and does not state that the protection factor reports on the free energy of local unfolding. The native-state pathway is described as small noncooperative fluctuations with no free energy assigned.
