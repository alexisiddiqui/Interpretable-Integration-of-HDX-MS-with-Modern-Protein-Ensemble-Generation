# 05 BioFeaturisers

Chapter hub for `thesis/chapters/05-biofeaturisers.tex`. The contribution is the library and the pipeline. BPTI tests basin coverage and retention against a long reference simulation, MoPrP tests pH-dependent structural responses under one HDX restraint, and TeaA tests population recovery from synthetic uptake.

## Chapter argument

- A library of differentiable forward models (JAX, with PyTorch wrappers): a Heaviside-contact BV model with usable gradients and an FFT SAXS model. One forward-model definition serves reweighting (jaxENT), restrained sampling (metadynamics–metainference) and guided generation (metadiffusion).
- [[BPTI]] against the 1 ms Anton trajectory, by metainference–metadynamics. MI+PB with ±0 residue exclusions first generates a diverse ensemble; four structures selected across that ensemble seed sampling with the correct ±2 exclusions, with matched MI+PB, MI-only and unbiased conditions. Each stage uses four 250 ns replicas, so one two-stage route totals 2 μs, 500-fold less aggregate production simulation than the 1 ms reference. The restraints use experimental HDX-NMR protection factors, and Anton supplies the controlled structural reference. The experiment tests whether restrained sampling recovers the basins and populations a long reference simulation shows; the existing PCA comparison establishes basin coverage and retention from the diverse starts. Protocol and comparison in [[bf-bpti]].
- MoPrP at pH 4 and pH 7: candidates from metadynamics–metainference, then unrestrained short apo simulations from decorrelated structures: tests whether pH-dependent partially unfolded states appear. Reading a pathway from them needs the trajectories, not the weights ([[conformational kinetics]]). The closest match to PUF1 is β1, the loop and α1 N displaced together (MSM ΔG 2.60 kcal/mol at pH 4 against 2.2 ± 0.2). The closest match to PUF2 lies in the same detached β1–α1 set with α1 and β2 also shifted (3.35 against 3.1 ± 0.5), although β2 shifts and does not detach. Both candidates rest on the 12 pH 7 #3 seeds, and MaxCal does not preserve this agreement (3.90 and 3.47, with the order inverted). The campaign shows pH differences in the MSM, where core distortion is 2.3–2.8 times and the α1–β2 unit shift 7–10 times more populated at pH 4 at both lags. These results depend on seeding, and constant-pH simulations are required to resolve these ambiguities. Mechanistic question; depends on protonation (ch. 6). Protocol and results in [[bf-moprp]].
- TeaA (two lobes joined by a hinge) against a synthetic HDX target built from MD frames at 40 % open : 60 % closed, by metadiffusion with a well-tempered bias along a hinge coordinate taken from Boltz-2's own fluctuations, then late-stage HDX guidance and MaxEnt reweighting: tests whether the open:closed populations are recovered with no MD reference in any run. The bias gives coverage of the hinge angle and the populations come from the reweighting. In the Fiedler-lobe run, where the hinge bodies also come from Boltz's pair representation, the refined ensemble puts 0.419 of the weight on the open state against a target of 0.40, and its strict recovery of 70.3 % compares with 48.6 % for the unguided control. Mixtures of in-state open and closed structures fit the target almost equally well at 0.40 and 0.50 open (MSE 0.00591 against 0.00573). Known populations, synthetic data; protocol in `kb/experiments/bf-teaa.md`.
- Boundary (from the §2.4 hub): [[Guided diffusion samples are not equilibrium-weighted unless a correction such as reweighting is applied.]], and a restraint is only as good as the forward model where the data are.
- "heavy-side" (tex comments) is Heaviside.

## Methods plan

One bullet per main-text paragraph, in six parts. The cases follow the two branches of the library. BPTI is a controlled comparison of restrained simulation against a long reference trajectory, MoPrP applies the same branch to a biological question, and TeaA introduces the generation branch. Main-text paragraphs keep every choice that changes what a result means, and reproduction detail goes to the appendix list at the end.

### 1. Shared forward-model interface (`sec:biofeat-interface`)

Sources: [[Metainference and parallel-bias metadynamics (BioFeaturisers)]] (force path, PyTorch wrapper); [[Sigmoid-switch BV forward model (BioFeaturisers)]].

- I1. One forward-model definition serves reweighting, restrained simulation and guided generation, because each needs the same chain from coordinates to observables to mismatch and back to derivatives. BioFeaturisers computes the observables (residue-wise ln P_f, peptide uptake or a scattering profile) from coordinates in JAX and returns vector–Jacobian products, so each consumer passes the gradient of its own mismatch with respect to the observables and receives the gradient with respect to its own variables. jaxENT supplies frame weights and forward-model parameters over fixed structures (`ch:jaxent`). PLUMED supplies coordinates at each MD step and receives an atomic force. A PyTorch generator supplies coordinates during denoising and receives a coordinate gradient through a custom autograd function. The forward model is therefore written and tested once, and the applications differ only in what they let the data change.

### 2. Smooth-contact HDX and SAXS (`sec:biofeat-bv`, `sec:biofeat-saxs`)

Sources: [[Sigmoid-switch BV forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]; [[Differentiable SAXS forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]. The BV and EX2 equations are in `sec:bg-hdx-forward-models` (`eq:bg-bv-feature`, `eq:bg-bv-average`) and `eq:bg-ex2`.

- F1. The BV model counts heavy-atom contacts and hydrogen bonds with hard distance cutoffs ([[@bestStructuralInterpretationHydrogen2006]]), so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates and has no gradient to follow. BioFeaturisers replaces each step with a sigmoid, s(d; r, b) = 1 / (1 + exp[−b(r − d)]), as in Wan et al. ([[@wanReconcilingSimulatedEnsembles2020]]), so predicted protection varies smoothly with every atom position, and a steep sigmoid recovers the hard cutoff. Pairs on the same chain within m residues of the probe are excluded from both counts, and m is a modelling choice that the BPTI case varies.
- F2. The ensemble average is taken in ln P_f, before exchange, so peptide uptake is that of the geometric-mean protection and differs from the mean of per-frame uptakes wherever protection varies between frames. At a steepness of 5 Å⁻¹ a contact switches over about 0.9 Å around its cutoff, so atoms near a cutoff carry fractional counts, and β coefficients calibrated with hard cutoffs do not transfer automatically.
- F3. The same interface carries a SAXS profile, which constrains overall size and shape where HDX constrains residue environments. The profile is the Debye sum with FoXS form factors for the displaced solvent and the hydration layer ([[@debyeZerstreuungRontgenstrahlen1915]], [[@schneidman-duhovnyFoXSWebServer2010]]). It is evaluated directly over atom pairs or, for large structures, on a grid by Fourier transform, so the cost scales with the grid rather than with the square of the atom count. No case in this chapter applies a SAXS restraint.

### 3. Restrained and enhanced sampling (one subsection, `sec:biofeat-metainference`, `sec:biofeat-metadynamics`)

Sources: [[Metainference and parallel-bias metadynamics (BioFeaturisers)]]; background in `sec:bg-restrained-simulation`.

- F4. Metainference restrains the average over replicas and infers an uncertainty for each observation alongside the coordinates ([[@bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016]]), so the data act at the ensemble level at which they were measured and inconsistent observations are down-weighted by the sampling itself. BioFeaturisers couples this restraint to the coordinates through the shared interface (`sec:biofeat-interface`). The force path and simulation settings are in `app:biofeat-simulation`, and uncertainty bounds in `tab:biofeat-mipb`.
- F5. A restraint changes which structures are favoured but does not carry replicas over barriers, so exploration needs an additional bias. Parallel-bias metadynamics builds a one-dimensional history-dependent bias on each of several collective variables and combines them with conditional weights for their coupling, with walkers sharing one history ([[@pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015]]). In MI+PB the total bias enters metainference as a reweighting term ([[@alamdariEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2021]]), which corrects the replica average that the restraint compares with the data. It does not turn pooled frames from a time-dependent bias into equilibrium populations, so restrained ensembles here supply coverage and candidates, and populations come from unbiased dynamics or reweighting.

### 4. Metadiffusion, refinement and reweighting (`sec:biofeat-metadiffusion`)

Sources: [[Hinge-coordinate metadiffusion (BioFeaturisers)]]; ensemble guidance and split fitting from `projects/3-BioFeaturisers/meta-diffusion` `src/maxent_fk/guidance/losses.py` (`_uptake_loss_pre`) and `config.py` (`n_split_replicates`, `split_train_fraction`).

- F6. In TeaA, HDX guidance applied at every denoising step left the ensemble at the Boltz intermediate because the per-atom gradient was incoherent across atoms. We therefore used a collective bias to expand inter-lobe motion before refining structures against HDX. The lobe division comes from the Boltz pair representation, which groups strongly coupled residues, and the hinge axis from relative lobe rotations in an unguided ensemble. The angle θ measures rotation about this axis without an MD reference or a hand-chosen lobe boundary (`eq:biofeat-theta`).
- F7. To expand coverage of θ, a history-dependent Gaussian bias displaces structures away from previously visited angles, with new deposits reduced where the bias is already high ([[@barducciWelltemperedMetadynamicsSmoothly2008]]). Biasing acts in a short window late in denoising, because earlier displacements are undone by subsequent denoising and the hinge ceases to move at lower noise. HDX guidance is switched off in this window. Continuation rounds re-noise previous structures into the window and retain the bias history, extending the sampled range. The displacement and bias equations are in `eq:biofeat-metad-move` and `eq:biofeat-metad-bias`, with window and cap diagnostics in `app:biofeat-metadiffusion-diagnostics`.
- F8. Hinge rotation alone does not determine the local packing measured by the HDX forward model. A θ-stratified subset is therefore re-denoised with ensemble-level HDX guidance. At each guided step, the denoised structures' ln P_f values are averaged under the current MaxEnt weights and converted to uptake. The squared uptake mismatch is differentiated with respect to every structure's coordinates, so each moves according to its contribution to the ensemble error. MaxEnt refits alternate with coordinate refinement (`sec:bg-maxent`, `ch:jaxent`), and guidance is scaled to the Boltz update to limit structural distortion. Refinement and fitting settings are in `tab:biofeat-metadiffusion`.
- F9. After refinement, structures below pLDDT 0.7 are removed and MaxEnt is fitted over the retained pool with a uniform prior. Summing weights over ranges of θ gives state populations. Guided samples are not equilibrium-weighted (`sec:bg-diffusion`), so populations come from this final fit and the sampling bias is never read as a free energy.

### 5. Case-study designs and controls

Sources: [[bf-bpti]], [[bf-moprp]], [[bf-teaa]].

- B1. BPTI asks whether restrained sampling recovers the basins of a long reference simulation from a small fraction of its simulated time. The restraints use 41 residue-level HDX-NMR protection factors, and the 1.03 ms Anton trajectory ([[@shawAtomiclevelCharacterizationStructural2010]]) supplies the structural reference and none of the targets. The design separates generating diverse candidates from sampling with the correct exclusions. MI+PB with ±0 exclusions, which remove only the probe residue from contact counting, generates the source ensemble, biased on the χ3 dihedrals of the three disulfides. Production uses ±2 exclusions, which also remove the two residues on either side.
- B2. Four structures selected across the source ensemble, one per replica, seed three matched conditions from identical coordinates. These are ±2 MI+PB, ±2 MI, and unbiased MD, which tests what the generated diversity retains under the force field alone. Each stage runs four 250 ns replicas, so one route of diversity stage and one ±2 condition totals 2 μs of production, 500-fold less simulated time than the Anton reference. All ensembles are projected without refitting onto the Anton Cα PCA, and occupancy of the second basin is followed pooled, per replica and in 10 ns windows. These densities come from uniform frame counts, so they measure coverage and retention, not populations.
- M1. MoPrP compares pH 4 with pH 7 under one experimental restraint. Both systems are built from the same AlphaFold3 model of mouse PrP 122–230 ([[@abramsonAccurateStructurePrediction2024]]) with protonation fixed at each nominal pH by PDB2PQR and PROPKA ([[@dolinskyPDB2PQRExpandingUpgrading2007]], [[@olssonPROPKA3ConsistentTreatment2011]]). Both are restrained by the same 77 residue-wise ln P_f values, recovered by exPfact from the HDX-MS data of Moulick et al. ([[@skinnerEstimatingConstraintsProtection2019]], [[@moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015]]). The paired simulations therefore compare how the force field at each protonation responds to one restraint.
- M2. MI+PB at each pH generates candidates, with the bias on five collective variables placed on the PUF features (β1–β2 sheet contacts, the displacements of β1 and of the α1–β2 unit, and the helicity of α1 and of the C-terminal end of α3). To follow how these states persist and connect, unbiased trajectories are seeded from three basins of each MI+PB ensemble on a Cartesian Cα PCA fitted to the 20 NMR models of 2L39. Twelve seeds per basin are each run at both target pH values, so every seed starts from the same frame at pH 4 and pH 7, for 12.48 μs of production in total.
- T1. TeaA asks whether metadiffusion recovers known populations with no MD structure in any run. The target is synthetic HDX uptake from MD frames at 40 % open and 60 % closed, made with the same forward model that guides the generation, so the comparison carries no forward-model error. Generation sees the sequence, Boltz-2's own MSA-conditioned prediction as its only template, and the uptake. The MD open and closed structures enter only the scoring.
- T2. An unguided control from the same seed structures is reweighted in the same way, so the gain from guidance is measured against the generator's own coverage. States come from θ (open below −5°, closed above +5°, otherwise intermediate), and recovery is 100(1 − √JSD) over the three states as in `ch:jaxent`. Both are evaluation measures and never enter a loss. Mixtures of open and closed structures close to the MD references, scored by the same forward model, show how sharply the fit itself distinguishes open fractions, against which the recovered populations are read.

### 6. MoPrP state assignment and dynamics (`sec:biofeat-moprp-analysis`)

Sources: [[bf-moprp]]; M3 also `projects/2-jaxENT/manuscript/report/results.tex:119`; M6 the ORIGIN of `figures/biofeat-moprp-flow-ext12`.

- M3. The runs are followed on two planes. The PCA plane places frames by global Cartesian displacement from the NMR models, so it reads directly against where the seeds were selected. The dihedral plane used for MoPrP in `ch:jaxent` plots the Ramachandran dihedral RMSD to 2L39 (dRMSD) against the dihedral deviation of β1, which separates local change in β1 from global disorder.
- M4. PUF1 and PUF2 are experimental forms defined by which segments lose protection ([[@moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015]]). PUF1 (ΔG_op 2.2 ± 0.2 kcal mol⁻¹) disorders β1, the β1–α1 loop and the C-terminal end of α3, and PUF2 (3.1 ± 0.5 kcal mol⁻¹) detaches α1 and β2 from the α2–α3 core. Candidate signatures in simulated frames are identified by segment displacement. Each frame is superposed on the disulfide-proximal core, and a segment counts as displaced beyond the 99th percentile of Folded frames, with its self-fit RMSD separating rigid-body displacement from disorder. Text rules take each description literally. The PUF1 candidate replaces α3 C with the N-terminal half of α1, which matches the segments that exchange with m_op 0.4, and the PUF2 candidate is α1 and β2 displaced with the core intact. The main-text table connects experimental descriptions to candidate signatures (`tab:biofeat-puf-definitions`), including the hypothesised joint opening of the PUF1 and PUF2 units in PUF3. Segment definitions and calibration are in `app:biofeat-moprp-assignment` and `tab:biofeat-moprp-segments`.
- M4a. Segment rules test candidate signatures independently and can overlap (`tab:biofeat-moprp-rules`). Exclusive structural clusters assign each frame once in a fixed priority order (`tab:biofeat-moprp-clusters`). The PUF2 cluster additionally requires β1 displacement and takes precedence over PUF1; Folded-like is the residual class. The structural names (Folded, β1-detached arm, detached β1–α1 set, core-distorted lobe) describe regions of the PCA plane (`tab:biofeat-moprp-regions`). Historical seed labels identify geometric circles (`tab:biofeat-moprp-basins`) and do not assign experimental PUF forms.
- M5. Seeded runs start away from equilibrium, so their frame counts reflect where they began. Markov state models (MSMs) estimate stationary populations from the transitions the runs make ([[@prinzMarkovModelsMolecular2011]]), here with 100 microstates from k-means on 10 Cartesian PCs fitted to unbiased MD and a reversible maximum-likelihood fit at a lag of 5 ns. Not every macrostate passed the Chapman–Kolmogorov test, so the MSM is used to estimate stationary populations and its transition rates are not interpreted (`app:biofeat-moprp-msm`). Opening free energies follow the EX2 relation from the stationary probabilities of open and closed states (`eq:bg-opening-free-energy`), which places them on the scale of the experimental values.
- M6. To describe the direction of motion separately from stationary populations, drift is measured directly as the mean displacement per nanosecond on each projection (`app:biofeat-moprp-drift`). Drift describes motion in the projection, not in the full coordinates. Because every seed runs at both pH values, the pH 7 minus pH 4 change in drift compares the same 72 seeds.

### Appendix (reproduction detail and supporting analyses)

- Smooth-contact BV. Parameter defaults, atom selections, the hydrogen-bond acceptor set and the synthetic amide-H placement with its correction ([[Sigmoid-switch BV forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]).
- SAXS. The six-profile expansion, the gridding and smoothing correction and the kernel details ([[Differentiable SAXS forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]).
- Simulation settings. The dynamics shared by BPTI and MoPrP (2 fs timestep, hydrogen-bond constraints, PME, 1.0 nm cutoffs, V-rescale and C-rescale coupling), metainference noise settings, deposition settings and walls, and the force path (`app:biofeat-simulation`). The force on each observable is contracted with JAX coordinate derivatives without forming the full Jacobian. The CV definitions (`tab:biofeat-cvs`) sit beside the bias settings (`tab:biofeat-mipb`).
- Metadiffusion coordinate (`app:biofeat-metadiffusion`, `eq:biofeat-theta`). The two lobes are the two signs of the Fiedler vector of a graph built from Boltz's pair representation, so residues that the trunk couples strongly fall in the same lobe. About 150 structures are drawn from noise without guidance and their medoid becomes the base. For each structure, lobe I is fitted onto the base and the rotation carrying the base's lobe II onto the structure's lobe II is written as a rotation vector. The hinge axis is the principal direction of these vectors, and θ is the rotation-vector component along the axis ([[Hinge-coordinate metadiffusion (BioFeaturisers)]]).
- Metadiffusion bias and refinement (`eq:biofeat-metad-bias`, `eq:biofeat-metad-move`, `tab:biofeat-metadiffusion`). In the bias window each residue moves with its Cα along −α (dV/dθ)(∂θ/∂x_Cα), capped per structure, and one Gaussian is deposited at each structure's θ with the well-tempered height h₀ exp(−V/k_BT_θ) ([[@barducciWelltemperedMetadynamicsSmoothly2008]]). The output pools rounds 2–4 and eight continuations, excluding the first round from an empty history. Each MaxEnt refit averages three replicates trained on random 80 % splits of the residues. Exact schedules, seeds and pLDDT filter counts are in the settings table ([[bf-teaa]]).
- Metadiffusion development diagnostics (`app:biofeat-metadiffusion-diagnostics`). Axis bootstrap against pool size, cap, window and guidance-weight comparisons, and their development-run provenance before the amide-H correction ([[bf-teaa]] §Settings that changed the outcome).
- BPTI. Construct, solvation and preparation, start selection, residue-scaled uncertainty bounds, the PCA implementation and second-basin boundary sensitivity ([[bf-bpti]]).
- MoPrP preparation (`app:biofeat-moprp`). Force field, solvent and buffer, the preparation ladder, seed accounting (spacing, extensions, unbiased replicas) and MI+PB settings ([[bf-moprp]]).
- MoPrP assignment (`app:biofeat-moprp-assignment`). Segment thresholds (`tab:biofeat-moprp-segments`), independent rules (`tab:biofeat-moprp-rules`), exclusive cluster priorities (`tab:biofeat-moprp-clusters`) and landscape regions (`tab:biofeat-moprp-regions`) sit together. Geometric seed circles remain in `tab:biofeat-moprp-basins` ([[bf-moprp]]).
- MoPrP dynamics. MSM model choices, implied timescales and Chapman–Kolmogorov tests are in `app:biofeat-moprp-msm`. Drift uses a 1 ns lag and bins with at least 200 frame pairs from at least three runs (`app:biofeat-moprp-drift`; [[bf-moprp]]).
- TeaA. Target construction, generator conditioning, matched unguided control and mixture scoring are in `app:biofeat-teaa-evaluation`. Lobe residue ranges and reference-angle comparisons are in `app:biofeat-teaa-checks`. The amide-H correction and checks remain with the BV specification (`app:biofeat-bv`; [[bf-teaa]]).

- [biofeat-bpti-seq2-controls](../../figures/biofeat-bpti-seq2-controls/fig.png) is retained in the experiment record. It shows that ±2 MI and MI+PB from the original starts remain concentrated in the main basin, motivating the diversity stage.

Paragraph order, one finding each. Sources: [[bf-bpti]]; per-replica values from `figures/biofeat-bpti-matched-starts/replica-basin-summary.csv`.

- P1. We first asked whether restrained sampling with ±0 exclusions supplies the structural diversity that sampling with ±2 exclusions lacks. Run from the original starts, ±2 MI and MI+PB stayed concentrated in the main Anton basin. The ±0 MI+PB source instead covers both principal basins of the 1.03 ms reference, with 48.0 % of its frames in the second basin (PC1 < −0.55) against 14.4 % for Anton. These biased frame counts establish coverage, not equilibrium populations, and the excess of second-basin frames is what makes the source a diverse pool of starts.
- P2. Restarting from four structures selected across the source tests whether that diversity survives the change to ±2 exclusions. Pooled over the four replicas, 44.5 % of ±2 MI+PB frames and 44.9 % of ±2 MI frames lie in the second basin, against 35.6 % for unbiased MD from the same starts, and over the final 50 ns these fall to 37.0, 39.2 and 22.4 %. MI alone matches MI+PB, so retention of the second basin does not require the disulfide bias.
- P3. The replica traces distinguish retention of starting diversity from subsequent visits, and most second-basin coverage was retained from the two starts that began there. Start 1 stays in the second basin under every condition, spending 90, 98 and 84 % of the final 50 ns there under MI+PB, MI and unbiased MD. Start 2 leaves at 151.5 ns without restraint, at 200.4 ns under MI+PB and at 217.3 ns under MI, and only under MI+PB does it return, occupying the second basin for 99.9 % of the final 10 ns. Start 3 begins in the main basin and crosses into the second late in the run under MI and unbiased MD, so transitions occur in both directions within 250 ns.
- P4. One route, the ±0 diversity stage followed by one ±2 condition, uses 2 μs of production and recovers both basin locations of a trajectory 500 times longer. The frame fractions do not carry over to populations. Two of the four starts begin in the second basin, and all three conditions keep 36–45 % of their frames there against 14 % for Anton, so the fractions reflect both the starting allocation and the subsequent dynamics. Restrained sampling from diverse starts therefore establishes coverage and retention. Estimating populations needs unbiased dynamics from the sampled states, which is how the MoPrP case is built.

4. Paths figure. β1 alone exchanges with the detached β1–α1 set, which almost never connects directly to Folded, and the lobe is a separate branch off Folded. The dihedral plane separates β1 detachment from the rest.
5. MSM figure. At pH 4 the PUF1 candidate lies at 2.60 against 2.2 ± 0.2 kcal/mol and the PUF2 candidate at 3.35 against 3.1 ± 0.5. Both rest on the pH 7 #3 seeds. The MSM makes β1 opening too easy (0.52 against 2.2 kcal/mol).
6. MSM figure. pH 7 has a metastable state with PUF2-like character on the arm at PC (87.5, 17.5), at 0.65 against 1.7 kcal/mol at pH 4, where 72 % of the MSM weight meets the PUF2 text rule (0 % at pH 4). It only just meets the PUF2 candidate definition, because β2 shifts by 0.28–0.33 nm against a 0.25 nm threshold rather than detaching, and it rests on the pH 7 #3 seeds. Core distortion (2.3–2.8 times) and the unit shift (7–10 times) are more populated at pH 4.
7. Flow figure. pH 7 has one clear slow corridor from Folded towards that metastable state on the arm, whereas pH 4 has a flat slow region that splits into two routes, towards the lobe and along the arm. These are the parallel pathways. Drift is measured in the projection, and early and late drift agree (median cosine 0.89–0.94), so runs of different lengths can be pooled.

Seeding from the detached β1–α1 set cannot show Folded opening into it. These results depend on seeding, and constant-pH simulations are required to resolve these ambiguities, together with better paired experiments.

## TeaA figures and section plan

- [biofeat-teaa-landscape-F](../../figures/biofeat-teaa-landscape-F/summary_1d.png) is the main-text TeaA figure, `summary_1d` (θ and PC1 densities for the MD 40:60 mixture, unguided Boltz, the metadiffusion union and the refined ensemble before and after MaxEnt) with `refmix_curve` (fit of in-state open and closed mixtures against open fraction, with the refined, union and control floors) as a second panel.
- `pca_logdensity` from the same set goes in the appendix. It gives every ensemble and the control on the MD Cα PCA plane, with the MD 95 % regions.

Paragraph order, one finding each. Sources: [[bf-teaa]] §6; `figures/biofeat-teaa-landscape-F/summary.csv`.

- P1. TeaA tests the generation branch, where the task changes from retaining diversity to creating it. Without guidance, Boltz-2 does not create it. The 150 structures drawn from noise lie within 2° of one hinge angle (θ from −2.2 to +1.8°), between the MD open (−18.4°) and closed (+9.7°) references, so the generator alone covers neither state.
- P2. The hinge bias supplies the missing coverage. Across rounds 2–4 and eight continuations the union of 550 structures spans θ from −41 to +25°, beyond both MD references, although no MD structure enters any run and both the bodies and the axis come from the generator's own pair representation and unguided fluctuations. The bias acts on θ alone, so the union provides coverage, and the populations come from what the HDX data do with it.
- P3. After refinement against the HDX target and MaxEnt reweighting, the ensemble puts 0.419 of its weight on open structures, 0.155 on intermediate and 0.426 on closed, against a target of 0.40 : 0 : 0.60. Strict recovery is 70.3 %, against 48.6 % for the control denoised without guidance from the same seed structures and reweighted in the same way (69.5 against 60.1 % at λ 100). The open weight peaks at −24 to −18°, around the open reference, while the closed weight sits at +12 to +24°, beyond the closed reference at +9.7°. The guidance therefore recovers the open population at the open hinge angle and places the closed population at a wider closure than MD.
- P4. Reweighting the union directly, without refinement, recovers 67.1 %, so the bias supplies most of what can be recovered along θ. Its structures fit the data poorly, however, with a floor of 0.0108 that is no better than the control (0.0110), and the weights over-open to 0.592 : 0.113 : 0.295. Refinement lowers the floor fourfold, to 0.0027, because re-denoising with guidance sets the packing within each lobe that the contact model reads. The open weight falls to 0.419 and recovery rises to 70.3 %, or from 77.1 to 91.8 % over the two states.
- P5. Recovery holds on θ, the coordinate the bias acts on, and does not extend to the full geometry. On the MD PCA plane, 9.8 % of the refined weight lies inside the MD open 95 % region and 7.7 % inside the closed one, against 40.0 and 59.5 % for the 40:60 MD mixture, because the refined structures spread beyond both states along PC1. The fit itself cannot tighten this. Mixtures of open and closed structures close to the MD references fit the target almost equally well at 0.40 and 0.50 open (MSE 0.00591 against a minimum of 0.00572 at 0.491), and the refined ensemble fits better than any of them (0.0027). Agreement with uptake therefore fixes the hinge populations without an MD reference, and it does not identify the structures that carry them.
