# 05 BioFeaturisers

Chapter hub for `thesis/chapters/05-biofeaturisers.tex`. The contribution is the library and the pipeline. BPTI tests basin coverage and retention against a long reference simulation, MoPrP tests pH-dependent structural responses under one HDX restraint, and TeaA tests population recovery from synthetic uptake.

## Chapter argument

- A library of differentiable forward models (JAX, with PyTorch wrappers): a Heaviside-contact BV model with usable gradients and an FFT SAXS model. One forward-model definition serves reweighting (jaxENT), restrained sampling (metadynamics–metainference) and guided generation (metadiffusion).
- [[BPTI]] against the 1 ms Anton trajectory, by metainference–metadynamics. Biasing the disulfide sidechain torsions with ±2 exclusions did not produce the backbone displacement into the left Anton basin. MI+PB with ±0 residue exclusions supplies the additional perturbation to generate a diverse ensemble; four structures selected across that ensemble seed sampling with the correct ±2 exclusions, with matched MI+PB, MI-only and unbiased conditions. Each stage uses four 250 ns replicas, so one two-stage route totals 2 μs, 500-fold less aggregate production simulation than the 1 ms reference. The restraints use experimental HDX-NMR protection factors, and Anton supplies the controlled structural reference. The experiment tests whether restrained sampling recovers the basins and populations a long reference simulation shows; the existing PCA comparison establishes basin coverage and retention from the diverse starts. Protocol and comparison in [[bf-bpti]].
- MoPrP at pH 4 and pH 7: candidates from metadynamics–metainference, then unrestrained short apo simulations from decorrelated structures: tests whether pH-dependent partially unfolded states appear. Reading a pathway from them needs the trajectories, not the weights ([[conformational kinetics]]). The closest match to PUF1 is β1, the loop and α1 N displaced together (MSM ΔG 2.60 kcal/mol at pH 4 against 2.2 ± 0.2). The closest match to PUF2 lies in the same detached β1–α1 set with α1 and β2 also shifted (3.35 against 3.1 ± 0.5), although β2 shifts and does not detach. Both candidates rest on the 12 pH 7 #3 seeds, and MaxCal does not preserve this agreement (3.90 and 3.47, with the order inverted). The campaign shows pH differences in the MSM, where core distortion is 2.3–2.8 times and the α1–β2 unit shift 7–10 times more populated at pH 4 at both lags. These results depend on seeding; constant-pH simulations address protonation–conformation coupling, while broader opening and return sampling tests kinetic access. The mechanistic thread is potential-gated unfolding and refolding (`fig:intro-frustrated-memory`): persistence and exchange after release depend on the sampled starting conformation, while a reproducible pH dependence of that access remains unresolved (own; [[bf-moprp]]). Protonation links this question to ch. 6. Protocol and results in [[bf-moprp]].
- TeaA (two lobes joined by a hinge) against a synthetic HDX target built from MD frames at 40 % open : 60 % closed, by metadiffusion with a well-tempered bias along a hinge coordinate taken from Boltz-2's own fluctuations, then late-stage HDX guidance and MaxEnt reweighting: tests whether the open:closed populations are recovered with no MD reference in any run. The bias gives coverage of the hinge angle and the populations come from the reweighting. In the Fiedler-lobe run, where the hinge bodies also come from Boltz's pair representation, the refined ensemble puts 0.419 of the weight on the open state against a target of 0.40, and its strict recovery of 70.3 % compares with 48.6 % for the unguided control. Mixtures of in-state open and closed structures fit the target almost equally well at 0.40 and 0.50 open (MSE 0.00591 against 0.00573). Known populations, synthetic data; protocol in `kb/experiments/bf-teaa.md`.
- Boundary (from the §2.4 hub): [[Guided diffusion samples are not equilibrium-weighted unless a correction such as reweighting is applied.]], and a restraint is only as good as the forward model where the data are.
- "heavy-side" (tex comments) is Heaviside.

## Methods plan

One bullet per main-text paragraph, in six parts. The cases follow the two branches of the library. BPTI is a controlled comparison of restrained simulation against a long reference trajectory, MoPrP applies the same branch to a biological question, and TeaA introduces the generation branch. Main-text paragraphs keep every choice that changes what a result means, and reproduction detail goes to the appendix list at the end.

### 1. Shared forward-model interface (`sec:biofeat-interface`)

Sources: [[Metainference and parallel-bias metadynamics (BioFeaturisers)]] (force path, PyTorch wrapper); [[Sigmoid-switch BV forward model (BioFeaturisers)]].

- I1. One forward-model definition serves reweighting, restrained simulation and guided generation, because each needs the same chain from coordinates to observables to mismatch and back to derivatives. BioFeaturisers computes the observables (residue-wise ln P_f, peptide uptake or a scattering profile) from coordinates in JAX and returns vector–Jacobian products, so each consumer passes the gradient of its own mismatch with respect to the observables and receives the gradient with respect to its own variables. jaxENT supplies frame weights and forward-model parameters over fixed structures (`ch:jaxent`). PLUMED supplies coordinates at each MD step and receives an atomic force. A PyTorch generator supplies coordinates during denoising and receives a coordinate gradient through a custom autograd function. The forward model is therefore written and tested once, and the applications differ only in what they let the data change.

### 2. Smooth-contact HDX and SAXS (`sec:biofeat-bv`, `sec:biofeat-saxs`)

Sources: [[Sigmoid-switch BV forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]; [[Differentiable SAXS forward model (BioFeaturisers)]]. The BV and EX2 equations are in `sec:bg-hdx-forward-models` (`eq:bg-bv-feature`, `eq:bg-bv-average`) and `eq:bg-ex2`.

- F1. Returning coordinate derivatives requires the forward model to vary smoothly with atom positions. The BV model instead counts heavy-atom contacts and hydrogen bonds with hard distance cutoffs ([[@bestStructuralInterpretationHydrogen2006]]), so its prediction is piecewise constant in the coordinates. BioFeaturisers replaces each step with a sigmoid, s(d; r, b) = 1 / (1 + exp[−b(r − d)]), as in Wan et al. ([[@wanReconcilingSimulatedEnsembles2020]]), so predicted protection varies smoothly with every atom position, and a steep sigmoid recovers the hard cutoff. At a steepness of 5 Å⁻¹ a contact switches over about 0.9 Å around its cutoff, so atoms near a cutoff carry fractional counts, and β coefficients calibrated with hard cutoffs do not transfer automatically. Pairs on the same chain within m residues of the probe are excluded from both counts, and m is a modelling choice that the BPTI case varies.
- F2. To predict ensemble uptake, protection is first combined across structures in ln P_f, before exchange is applied. Peptide uptake is therefore that of the geometric-mean protection and differs from the mean of per-frame uptakes wherever protection varies between frames.
- F3. The same interface carries a SAXS profile, which constrains overall size and shape where HDX constrains residue environments. The profile is the Debye sum with FoXS form factors for the displaced solvent and the hydration layer ([[@debyeZerstreuungRontgenstrahlen1915]], [[@schneidman-duhovnyFoXSWebServer2010]]). It is evaluated directly over atom pairs or, for large structures, on a grid by Fourier transform, so the cost scales with the grid rather than with the square of the atom count. No case in this chapter applies a SAXS restraint.

### 3. Restrained and enhanced sampling (one subsection, `sec:biofeat-metainference`, `sec:biofeat-metadynamics`)

Sources: [[Metainference and parallel-bias metadynamics (BioFeaturisers)]]; background in `sec:bg-restrained-simulation`.

- F4. To use ensemble HDX predictions during simulation, we coupled the forward model to metainference through the shared interface (`sec:biofeat-interface`). Metainference restrains the average over replicas and infers an uncertainty for each observation alongside the coordinates ([[@bonomiMetadynamicMetainferenceEnhanced2016]]), so the data act at the ensemble level at which they were measured and inconsistent observations are down-weighted by the sampling itself. The force path and simulation settings are in `app:biofeat-simulation`, and uncertainty bounds in `tab:biofeat-mipb`.
- F5. A restraint changes which structures are favoured but does not carry replicas over barriers, so exploration needs an additional bias. Parallel-bias metadynamics builds a one-dimensional history-dependent bias on each of several collective variables and combines them with conditional weights for their coupling, with walkers sharing one history ([[@pfaendtnerEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2015]]). In MI+PB the total bias enters metainference as a reweighting term ([[@alamdariEfficientSamplingHighDimensional2021]]), which corrects the replica average that the restraint compares with the data. It does not turn pooled frames from a time-dependent bias into equilibrium populations, so restrained ensembles here supply coverage and candidates, and populations come from unbiased dynamics or reweighting.

### 4. Metadiffusion, refinement and reweighting (`sec:biofeat-metadiffusion`)

Sources: [[Hinge-coordinate metadiffusion (BioFeaturisers)]]; ensemble guidance and split fitting from `projects/3-BioFeaturisers/meta-diffusion` `src/maxent_fk/guidance/losses.py` (`_uptake_loss_pre`) and `config.py` (`n_split_replicates`, `split_train_fraction`).

- F6. In diffusion generation, we likewise separated exploration of collective motion from refinement against HDX. In TeaA, HDX guidance applied at every denoising step left the ensemble at the Boltz intermediate because the per-atom gradient was incoherent across atoms. We therefore used a collective bias to expand inter-lobe motion before refining structures against HDX. The lobe division comes from the Boltz pair representation, which groups strongly coupled residues, and the hinge axis from relative lobe rotations in an unguided ensemble. The angle θ measures rotation about this axis without an MD reference or a hand-chosen lobe boundary (`eq:biofeat-theta`).
- F7. To expand coverage of θ, a history-dependent Gaussian bias displaces structures away from previously visited angles, with new deposits reduced where the bias is already high ([[@barducciWelltemperedMetadynamicsSmoothly2008]]). Biasing acts in a short window late in denoising, because earlier displacements are undone by subsequent denoising and the hinge ceases to move at lower noise. HDX guidance is switched off in this window. Continuation rounds re-noise previous structures into the window and retain the bias history, extending the sampled range. The displacement and bias equations are in `eq:biofeat-metad-move` and `eq:biofeat-metad-bias`, with window and cap diagnostics in `app:biofeat-metadiffusion-diagnostics`.
- F8. Hinge rotation alone does not determine the local packing measured by the HDX forward model. A θ-stratified subset is therefore re-denoised with ensemble-level HDX guidance. At each guided step, the denoised structures' ln P_f values are averaged under the current MaxEnt weights and converted to uptake. The squared uptake mismatch is differentiated with respect to every structure's coordinates, so each moves according to its contribution to the ensemble error. MaxEnt refits alternate with coordinate refinement (`sec:bg-maxent`, `ch:jaxent`), and guidance is scaled to the Boltz update to limit structural distortion. Refinement and fitting settings are in `tab:biofeat-metadiffusion`.
- F9. Guided samples are not equilibrium-weighted (`sec:bg-diffusion`), so state populations were estimated by a final MaxEnt fit. After refinement, structures below pLDDT 0.7 were removed and MaxEnt was fitted over the retained pool with a uniform prior. Summing weights over ranges of θ gives state populations. The sampling bias is never read as a free energy.

### 5. Case-study designs and controls

Sources: [[bf-bpti]], [[bf-moprp]], [[bf-teaa]].

- BPTI tests recovery of reference basins, MoPrP tests the response to a shared HDX restraint under different protonation states, and TeaA tests recovery of known populations from guided generation.
- B1. BPTI asks whether restrained sampling recovers the basins of a long reference simulation from a small fraction of its simulated time. The restraints use 41 residue-level HDX-NMR protection factors, and the 1.03 ms Anton trajectory ([[@shawAtomiclevelCharacterizationStructural2010]]) supplies the structural reference and none of the targets. The design separates generating diverse candidates from sampling with the correct exclusions. MI+PB with ±0 exclusions, which remove only the probe residue from contact counting, generates the source ensemble, biased on the χ3 dihedrals of the three disulfides. Production uses ±2 exclusions, which also remove the two residues on either side.
- B2. Four structures selected across the source ensemble, one per replica, seed three matched conditions from identical coordinates. These are ±2 MI+PB, ±2 MI, and unbiased MD, which tests what the generated diversity retains under the force field alone. Each stage runs four 250 ns replicas, so one route of diversity stage and one ±2 condition totals 2 μs of production, 500-fold less simulated time than the Anton reference. All ensembles are projected without refitting onto the Anton Cα PCA, and occupancy of the second basin is followed pooled, per replica and in 10 ns windows. These densities come from uniform frame counts, so they measure coverage and retention, not populations.
- M1. MoPrP compares pH 4 with pH 7 under one experimental restraint. Both systems are built from the same AlphaFold3 model of mouse PrP 122–230 ([[@abramsonAccurateStructurePrediction2024]]) with protonation fixed at each nominal pH by PDB2PQR and PROPKA ([[@dolinskyPDB2PQRExpandingUpgrading2007]], [[@olssonPROPKA3ConsistentTreatment2011]]). Both are restrained by the same 77 residue-wise ln P_f values, recovered by exPfact from the HDX-MS data of Moulick et al. ([[@skinnerEstimatingConstraintsProtection2019]], [[@moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015]]). The paired simulations therefore compare how the force field at each protonation responds to one restraint.
- M2. MI+PB at each pH generates candidates, with the bias on five collective variables placed on the PUF features (β1–β2 sheet contacts, the displacements of β1 and of the α1–β2 unit, and the helicity of α1 and of the C-terminal end of α3). To follow how these states persist and connect, unbiased trajectories are seeded from three basins of each MI+PB ensemble on a Cartesian Cα PCA fitted to the 20 NMR models of 2L39. Twelve seeds per basin are each run at both target pH values, so every seed starts from the same frame at pH 4 and pH 7, for 12.48 μs of production in total.
- T1. TeaA asks whether metadiffusion recovers known populations with no MD structure in any run. The target is synthetic HDX uptake from MD frames at 40 % open and 60 % closed, made with the same forward model that guides the generation, so the comparison carries no forward-model error. Generation sees the sequence, Boltz-2's own MSA-conditioned prediction as its only template, and the uptake. The MD open and closed structures enter only the scoring.
- T2. An unguided control from the same seed structures is reweighted in the same way, so the gain from guidance is measured against the generator's own coverage. States come from θ (open below −5°, closed above +5°, otherwise intermediate), and recovery is 100(1 − √JSD) over the three states as in `ch:jaxent`. Both are evaluation measures and never enter a loss. Mixtures of open and closed structures close to the MD references, scored by the same forward model, show how sharply the fit itself distinguishes open fractions, against which the recovered populations are read.

### 6. MoPrP state assignment and dynamics (`sec:biofeat-moprp-analysis`)

Sources: [[bf-moprp]]; M3 also `projects/2-jaxENT/manuscript/report/results.tex:119`; M6 the ORIGIN of `figures/biofeat-moprp-flow-ext12`.

- M3. The runs are followed on two planes. The PCA plane places frames by global Cartesian displacement from the NMR models, so it reads directly against where the seeds were selected. The dihedral plane used for MoPrP in `ch:jaxent` plots the Ramachandran dihedral RMSD to 2L39 (dRMSD) against the dihedral deviation of β1, which separates local change in β1 from global disorder.
- M4. These projections describe global and local conformational change, while the experimental PUF1 and PUF2 descriptions identify which segments lose protection ([[@moulickPartiallyUnfoldedForms2015]]). PUF1 (ΔG_op 2.2 ± 0.2 kcal mol⁻¹) disorders β1, the β1–α1 loop and the C-terminal end of α3, and PUF2 (3.1 ± 0.5 kcal mol⁻¹) detaches α1 and β2 from the α2–α3 core. To test those descriptions in simulated frames, we measured how far each segment moved relative to the core. Each frame is superposed on the disulfide-proximal core, and a segment counts as displaced beyond the 99th percentile of Folded frames, with its self-fit RMSD separating rigid-body displacement from disorder. Text rules take each description literally. The PUF1 candidate replaces α3 C with the N-terminal half of α1, which matches the segments that exchange with m_op 0.4, and the PUF2 candidate is α1 and β2 displaced with the core intact. The main-text table connects experimental descriptions to candidate signatures (`tab:biofeat-puf-definitions`), including the hypothesised joint opening of the PUF1 and PUF2 units in PUF3. Segment definitions and calibration are in `app:biofeat-moprp-assignment` and `tab:biofeat-moprp-segments`.
- M4a. Segment rules test candidate signatures independently and can overlap (`tab:biofeat-moprp-rules`). Exclusive structural clusters assign each frame once in a fixed priority order (`tab:biofeat-moprp-clusters`). The PUF2 cluster additionally requires β1 displacement and takes precedence over PUF1; Folded-like is the residual class. The structural names (Folded, β1-detached arm, detached β1–α1 set, core-distorted lobe) describe regions of the PCA plane (`tab:biofeat-moprp-regions`). Historical seed labels identify geometric circles (`tab:biofeat-moprp-basins`) and do not assign experimental PUF forms.
- M5. Seeded runs start away from equilibrium, so their frame counts reflect where they began. Markov state models (MSMs) estimate stationary populations from the transitions the runs make ([[@prinzMarkovModelsMolecular2011]]), here with 100 microstates from k-means on 10 Cartesian PCs fitted to unbiased MD and a reversible maximum-likelihood fit at a lag of 5 ns. Not every macrostate passed the Chapman–Kolmogorov test, so the MSM is used to estimate stationary populations and its transition rates are not interpreted (`app:biofeat-moprp-msm`). Opening free energies follow the EX2 relation from the stationary probabilities of open and closed states (`eq:bg-opening-free-energy`), which places them on the scale of the experimental values.
- M6. To describe the direction of motion separately from stationary populations, drift is measured directly as the mean displacement per nanosecond on each projection (`app:biofeat-moprp-drift`). Drift describes motion in the projection, not in the full coordinates. Because every seed runs at both pH values, the pH 7 minus pH 4 change in drift compares the same 72 seeds.

## Results narrative

The results ask what changing coordinates adds to fitting a fixed ensemble. BPTI establishes reference-basin coverage and retention, MoPrP follows the structural candidates after release from restraint, and TeaA tests whether guided generation and reweighting recover known state weights. BPTI leads into MoPrP because retained diversity still needs a population analysis. TeaA changes the sampling mechanism and returns to a synthetic target to separate coverage, refinement and population recovery. The library includes SAXS, while these cases demonstrate HDX-driven applications.

Each figure introduces a comparison with an observable expectation. BPTI asks whether both reference regions remain occupied after restart. MoPrP asks which segments move together, which sampled states connect and how their behaviour changes between paired pH runs. TeaA asks whether weight moves from the unguided intermediate towards the reference states, then whether this agreement extends beyond the hinge coordinate. Counts, stationary probabilities and fitted weights retain their distinct meanings throughout.

We therefore asked whether short restrained simulations of BPTI recover the basins sampled by the 1.03 ms Anton trajectory. BPTI supplies experimental HDX-NMR protection factors for the restraints and a separate long simulation against which to assess structural coverage. ## MoPrP figures and section plan

Native-state HDX identifies at least two partially unfolded forms of MoPrP at pH 4. The denaturant dependence of exchange groups residues into subglobal opening events and supplies opening free energies, but leaves the structural arrangements underlying those openings unresolved. HDX-restrained sampling proposes candidate conformations; release from restraint tests their persistence and return towards the native fold (`thesis/chapters/05-biofeaturisers.tex` L159).

MoPrP provides a test of the condition-dependent unfolding and refolding introduced in `fig:intro-frustrated-memory` (`thesis/chapters/01-introduction.tex` L78–84). At pH 4, experimental unfolding and refolding follow distinct routes and their kinetics depend on initial conditions; pH and urea together alter access between native and denatured ensembles ([[At pH 4 the mouse prion protein folds along a pathway distinct from its unfolding pathway, so its kinetics depend on initial conditions.]]). The thesis interprets this behaviour through a potential-gated landscape, in which an applied condition changes the barriers between conformations and the state retained records the history of that condition ([[Because folding and unfolding follow distinct pathways at pH 4, the prion landscape can retain the history of the condition applied, which reads as a form of memory.]]). Here we ask whether partially opened structures persist after release from restraint, which rearrangements permit return towards the native fold, and whether changing protonation changes access between them. Persistence and restricted exchange in the simulations supply the structural basis for examining this gating hypothesis (own; [[bf-moprp]]).

Protonation gives the proposed gate a physical basis. H186 protonation and disruption of the R155–D201 salt bridge destabilise MoPrP, and experimental HDX locates increased dynamics around α1 and the α1–β2 loop ([[Protonation of H186 or D201 drives MoPrP misfolding, and HDX-MS shows helix 1 and the α1–β2 loop become more dynamic.]]). We therefore examine how β1, α1 and β2 move relative to one another and the core, distinguishing displacement of a structured element from its internal unfolding. HDX-derived partially unfolded forms supply segment-level hypotheses and experimental opening free energies for these comparisons. The physical question is which structural changes can persist and interconvert once the force that generated them is removed ([[bf-moprp]]).

Constant-pH simulations of human PrP provide a precedent for protonation-dependent access to alternative conformations: His187 protonation, corresponding to mouse H186, separates the subdomains, and one trajectory at pH 2 forms a stable β-rich structure ([[Constant-pH MD of human PrP at low pH populates a β-rich form in which His187 protonation separates the subdomains.]]). This coupling motivates pH as a possible gating potential, with the human pH 2 result providing context for the mouse pH 4 and pH 7 comparison (`thesis/chapters/05-biofeaturisers.tex` L163).

## TeaA figures and section plan

In BPTI and MoPrP, experimental restraints and enhanced sampling supplied conformations that could then be assessed against reference structures or followed after release from restraint. The same division of work applies to diffusion generation. A collective bias can expand the conformations proposed by the generator, while HDX guidance refines their contribution to an ensemble explanation. We therefore asked whether this combination could recover a known conformational mixture when the generator alone failed to cover its constituent states (own; [[bf-bpti]], [[bf-moprp]], [[bf-teaa]]).

TeaA provides this test through an opening and closing motion between two lobes. We constructed a synthetic HDX target from 40 % open and 60 % closed MD frames, using the same forward model for target construction and guidance. The known mixture allows us to distinguish improved uptake agreement from recovery of the populations that produced it. MD structures supplied the target and evaluation references but never entered generation ([[bf-teaa]]).

The design separates creating coverage, refining local environments and fitting populations. Unguided Boltz-2 establishes the generator's starting coverage; a hinge bias tests whether generation can reach beyond it. HDX-guided refinement is compared with unguided re-denoising from the same expanded seed pool, followed by the same MaxEnt reweighting, to isolate the contribution of the HDX guidance. Recovery is assessed against the known state weights and reference geometries, because a broader hinge distribution alone does not establish either. Mixtures of reference-like generated structures then test how sharply uptake agreement distinguishes open fractions, placing the recovered populations within the resolving power of the target ([[bf-teaa]] §6).

Uptake agreement neither uniquely fixes the state fractions nor identifies the structures that carry them.
