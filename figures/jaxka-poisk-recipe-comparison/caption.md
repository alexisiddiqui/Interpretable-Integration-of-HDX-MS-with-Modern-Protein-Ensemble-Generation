Comparison of GQT training recipes and structural inputs for distinguishing POISK binders from matched decoys. Each row uses a different score to rank the two antibodies against the same antigen. Pairwise accuracy measures how often the binder scores higher, with ties contributing one half; values above the dashed line at 0.5 exceed chance. Colours distinguish the structural information supplied to GQT. Blue uses independently predicted antibody and antigen structures (ABodyBuilder2 and ESMFold, respectively; 11,111 complete pairs). Orange uses partners extracted from Protenix-v2 complexes and processed separately, retaining their bound shapes but withholding their relative placement. Green supplies the complete Protenix-v2 complex, including the partners' relative placement (11,115 pairs for both orange and green). Symbols distinguish the baseline run4 model (hollow circles), training with contact ranking and hotspot masking, hmask (hollow diamonds), and subsequent burial-weighted contact training, dRSA (filled circles). Lines connect these recipes within each input condition; all use seed 17. Scores summarise predicted antibody–antigen contacts, solvent exposure or burial upon binding, using either the whole antibody variable fragment (Fv) or its complementarity-determining regions (CDRs). Contact scores use sums, maxima or the mean of the 50 highest probabilities. Weighted scores give greater weight to contacts between exposed residues or residues predicted to become buried; CDR shares measure the fraction of the corresponding whole-Fv score contributed by the CDRs. Dotted and solid vertical lines show published Boltz-2 and Protenix-v2 ipSAE accuracies, respectively, evaluated on the larger 11,247-pair benchmark. With independently predicted structures, top-50 CDR contact accuracy rises from 0.437 for run4 to 0.712 for hmask and 0.713 for dRSA. This discrimination alone does not establish whether the scores recognise the supplied antigen or exploit sequence differences between binders and decoys.
